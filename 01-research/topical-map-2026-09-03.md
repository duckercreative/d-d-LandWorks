## 1. Executive Summary

D&D Land Works is a **greenfield** local SEO build. `ddlandworks.com` does **not resolve to a live site** (verified 2026-09-03 via direct fetch and browser — no page loaded), and no Google Business Profile, Facebook, or Instagram presence is findable under the business name. Nothing exists to optimize; everything is built from zero. That is the clean case.

The strategy centers on **one entity: an Excavation & Landworks Contractor serving Lane County**, and refuses to dilute it. Every core service the business actually performs gets a strong, single-intent page; the site earns topical authority through depth and internal linking, not page count.

Three findings shape the plan:

1. **Location-page whitespace.** The strongest local competitors — Danco, All Dirt, Heavy D — rank on service pages but publish **no genuine city-level pages**; they merely list towns on the homepage. A disciplined set of real city and city×service pages for Eugene and Springfield is the single biggest ranking opportunity, provided they carry unique local value and avoid doorway-page patterns.
2. **Septic is a moat.** D&D is **DEQ Certified**, which legally qualifies it for septic install/repair. Competitors treat septic as a marquee cluster (All Dirt splits it into installation, drainfields, advanced treatment). D&D should own a focused septic cluster — install & repair only, no fabricated pumping/inspection/engineering claims.
3. **Scope discipline protects trust.** Competitors offer tree removal, stump grinding, forestry mulching, hauling, and asphalt paving. D&D's verified list does **not** include these. The map explicitly fences them out. Claiming them would be false and would blur the entity.

Recommended footprint at maturity: **~11 core/support service pages, 1 commercial audience page, a service-areas hub + county page, 10 city pages (phased), and a tight set of Tier-1 city×service pages** — plus a small, high-intent informational/cost layer. Not hundreds of pages.

## 9. Residential vs Commercial Architecture

**Rule: do not duplicate every service as "residential X" and "commercial X."** That creates cannibalization and doorway pages.

- **Residential = default.** The homepage and every service page speak to homeowners first (the volume audience) and mention commercial capability.
- **Commercial = one dedicated page** `/commercial-excavation/` `[RECOMMENDED]` **P1** — distinct intent (developers, GCs, larger scope, phased sitework, subdivisions, commercial pads). It links down to the same core service pages rather than cloning them.
- Residential/commercial nuance otherwise lives as a **section within each service page**, not as separate URLs.

## 15. Internal Linking Architecture

**Hub-and-spoke, reinforced by geo and problem links. Vary anchor text; avoid exact-match stuffing.**

- **Homepage →** the P0 core service pages + service-areas hub + contact.
- **Site Preparation (umbrella) →** land clearing, grading, foundation excavation, drainage (and back up to home).
- **Each service page →** its cluster parent (home or site prep), 1–2 sibling services, the most relevant city×service page, and contact.
- **Service-areas hub →** Lane County + every city page.
- **Lane County →** all city pages; **each city page →** the services offered there + adjacent city pages (Eugene↔Springfield; Creswell↔Cottage Grove).
- **City×service page →** parent city page + parent service page (both directions) — this is what stops city×service pages from becoming orphaned doorways.
- **Informational/cost article →** the commercial service page it supports (e.g. "excavation cost" → `/excavation/`; "fix standing water" → `/drainage-excavation/`).
- **Commercial page →** each core service (commercial section) + contact.

**Rule:** every spoke links to its hub; every hub links down to ≥3 spokes; every city×service page links to both its city and its service parent.

## 20. Final Recommendations

1. **Confirm the five open facts before build:** exact base city, whether `ddlandworks.com` is registered/parked/unbuilt, GBP status, service radius (esp. Florence), and the logo file. These gate Phase 1.
2. **Guard the entity.** One central identity — excavation & landworks contractor. Septic is a strong branch, not the trunk; never let it or any city page dominate.
3. **Win on location pages, carefully.** The competitor gap is the biggest opportunity, but only real, locally-specific Tier-1 city and city×service pages earn it. Everything Tier-2/3 stays city-only until it can say something true and local.
4. **Never claim unverified services.** Tree/stump removal, forestry mulching, hauling, paving, concrete, utility *installation*, septic *pumping*, structural/engineering guarantees are all out. Frame utility and drainage work as "excavation **for**…".
5. **Local SEO ≠ just pages.** Pair this map with GBP creation, `LocalBusiness`-subtype schema (verify type), consistent NAP, a review pipeline, and before/after project photos — the map ranks far better once those exist.
6. **Fewer, stronger pages.** ~25–35 quality pages at maturity, phased — not a mass-produced city×service grid.

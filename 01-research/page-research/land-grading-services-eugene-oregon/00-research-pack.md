## 8. FAQ Shortlist (6–8 questions for schema markup)

1. **Do I need a permit to grade my property in Lane County?**  
   → Yes, if your project moves more than 50 cubic yards of earth or creates a cut or fill slope steeper than 2:1. Contact Lane County Land Management Division at 3050 N. Delta Hwy, Eugene (M–F 9am–3pm). Foundation excavation under an active building permit is generally exempt; driveway and landscaping grading is not.

2. **How much does land grading cost in Eugene, Oregon?**  
   → National cost ranges are $0.08–$2.00 per square foot for yard grading, $1,000–$5,125 for driveway grading, and $1,000–$6,700 for foundation prep. Eugene area projects vary based on lot size, soil type, slope, and whether fill material is needed. Contact D&D Land Works at 541-401-8726 for a free site-specific estimate.

3. **What is the difference between rough grading and finish grading?**  
   → Rough grading is the bulk earthmoving stage — we set the building pad elevation, strip topsoil, and rough in drainage falls, typically within ±0.1 foot of the plan. Finish grading follows after underground utilities are installed; it refines the surface to ±0.5 inch precision and prepares the site for paving, planting, or final inspection.

4. **Do you call Oregon 811 before starting grading work?**  
   → Yes. Oregon law requires contractors to notify Oregon 811 (the Utility Notification Center) at least two business days before any excavation or grading begins. We submit this locate request as a standard part of every job before equipment touches the ground.

5. **Can grading fix standing water in my Eugene yard?**  
   → Often yes. Water pooling in Willamette Valley yards is frequently caused by flat or reverse-sloped grade — particularly where Malpass clay soil reduces drainage. We can regrade to direct water away from your home and toward proper outlets. On sites with heavy clay soil, we may recommend combining slope correction with a French drain or drainage swale for complete resolution.

6. **When is the best time to schedule grading in Oregon?**  
   → May through September is ideal — dry conditions reduce erosion risk and keep project costs lower. Grading can be done October through April but Oregon's wet season (Oct 1 – Apr 30) requires additional erosion control measures, which adds time and cost. Contact us early — spring/summer slots book quickly.

7. **How do I verify D&D Land Works' contractor license?**  
   → D&D Land Works holds CCB License #261742 with the Oregon Construction Contractors Board. You can verify our active license status, bond, and insurance at orccb.org. Look for both an Active license status and a current bond — a license can show Active even if the bond has separately lapsed.

8. **What is a building pad and do I need one?**  
   → A building pad is a leveled, compacted ground platform at a precise engineered elevation — required before any structure (home, shop, barn, ADU) can be built. It's different from general yard leveling: a pad must meet specific elevation specs set by your architect or engineer. D&D grades building pads for residential homes, outbuildings, and commercial structures across Lane County.

## 1. Lane County Grading & Fill Permit Requirements

**Authority:** Lane County Land Management Division (LMD)  
**Contact:** 3050 N. Delta Hwy, Eugene, OR 97408 | M–F 9:00am–3:00pm  
**Online portal:** aca-oregon.accela.com/LANE_CO/  

### Threshold Trigger
A grading permit is required in Lane County when a project involves:
- Moving **more than 50 cubic yards** of earth, OR
- Creating a cut or fill slope **steeper than 2:1** (horizontal:vertical)

### Permit Types (Lane County)
- **Type I:** Single-family residential projects or minor grading under 1 acre
- **Type II:** Larger developments, slopes greater than 15%, or highly erodible soils

### Exemptions
- Excavation for a building foundation that is authorized under a **valid building permit** is typically exempt from a standalone grading permit
- However, **driveway grading and landscaping grading are NOT exempt** — they require a permit if they exceed the 50 cu yd threshold

### Non-Urban vs Urban Fringe
- Any soil disturbance in the County **MS4 Area** (generally the urban fringe around Eugene/Springfield) requires erosion and stormwater permits regardless of volume

### Processing Timeline
- Residential (standard): 6–8 weeks once application is complete
- Simple projects: 3–4 weeks; timelines fluctuate seasonally (spring = slower)

### Content Signal
No Eugene competitor currently mentions the 50 cu yd threshold or explains permit types. Adding this to the D&D page establishes **regulatory authority** and answers a real homeowner anxiety before they call.

## 2. Oregon 811 — Utility Locate Requirements

**Authority:** Oregon Utility Notification Center (OUNC) / Dig Safely Oregon  
**Call:** 811 (toll-free) or submit online at Oregon811.com  
**Notice Requirement:** At least **2 business days** before any excavation or grading work begins  

### What Counts as "Excavation" in Oregon
Oregon OSHA defines excavation as "any operation in which earth, rock, or other material is moved or displaced by any means, including graders, backhoes, bulldozers, trenchers, augers, drilling machines, blasting, and all other powered equipment."  
This definition **explicitly includes grading work.**

### Exemption (Narrow)
Sidewalk, road, or ditch maintenance less than **12 inches in depth** that does not lower the original grade or original flow line of the ditch is exempt. For all other grading — 811 must be called.

### Employer OSHA Obligation
Oregon OSHA requires employers (i.e., the contractor) to:
1. Notify OUNC at least 2 business days before starting excavation
2. Estimate the location of utilities before opening/extending excavation
3. Determine the **exact location by safe means** as excavation approaches estimated utility locations
4. Protect, support, or remove underground installations to safeguard employees

### Why This Matters for Homeowners
Hiring a contractor who doesn't follow 811 procedures puts the homeowner at risk for utility damage costs. D&D Land Works following Oregon 811 is a trust signal that should be stated explicitly on the page.

## 3. Oregon CCB License Verification

**Authority:** Oregon Construction Contractors Board (CCB)  
**Verify at:** orccb.org  
**D&D Land Works CCB#:** 261742  

### What CCB Requires for Excavation Contractors
- Active CCB registration (license)
- Current surety bond (residential: $10,000–$20,000; commercial: $20,000–$75,000)
- General liability insurance
- Workers' compensation (if employees)

### Verification Note
A license can show "Active" while the bond has separately expired. CCB lookup at orccb.org shows both license and bond status. Consumers should verify both.

## 5. Willamette Valley Soil Types and Grading Implications

### Malpass Clay (West Eugene / Willamette Valley Wetlands)
**Scientific Classification:** Volcanic ash from Mt. Mazama (Crater Lake caldera); weathered into smectitic gray imogolite and clay via X-ray diffraction-confirmed process.  
**USDA Classification:** Hydric soil; listed in Eugene-area BLM/NRCS soil surveys  
**Source:** BLM Technical Note 447 (U.S. Bureau of Land Management), Cascadia Prairie-Oak Partnership  

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Permeability | Very slow — water moves extremely slowly through Malpass clay |
| Shrink/Swell | High — shrinks and cracks when dry; swells and seals when wet |
| Winter behavior | Swells → creates impermeable surface → ponding |
| Summer behavior | Dries → cracks open — can damage graded surfaces |
| Drainage impact | Surfaces appear level but pool water; subsurface drainage inadequate |
| Grading implication | Standard slope grading alone may be insufficient; French drains or drainage swales often required |
| Distribution | Dominant in West Eugene Wetlands; widespread in Willamette Valley bottomlands |

**Content Signal:** Malpass clay is mentioned by zero competitors. Explaining it on the page establishes hyperlocal soil expertise that no national content farm can replicate. It directly answers "why does my Eugene yard pool water every winter."

### Jory Soil (Willamette Valley Foothills — Contrast Soil)
**Classification:** Oregon's state soil; NRCS official designation  
**Texture:** Very deep, well-drained, silty clay loam; red-orange from iron oxidation  
**Construction behavior:** Stable, well-drained, good load-bearing characteristics  
**Grading implication:** Jory soil sites generally drain better; grading for slope alone may be sufficient without additional drainage systems  
**Distribution:** Mapped on 300,000+ acres in western Oregon, primarily foothills around Willamette Valley  

**Note for D&D:** Jory soil appears more in the higher-elevation foothills (Creswell, Cottage Grove area) vs the valley floor (Eugene, Springfield). West Eugene clients on bottomland are more likely dealing with Malpass clay conditions.

## 6. Oregon Wet Season — Grading Schedule Impact

**Wet Season Definition:** October 1 – April 30 (Oregon construction standard)  
Note: Oregon ODOT defines it as October 1 – May 30 for their projects  

### Erosion Control Requirements (Oct 1 – Apr 30)
- All **denuded (exposed) soil must be stabilized within 2 days** of ground-disturbing activity
- Methods: hydro seeding, mulching, compost, plastic sheeting with anchors
- An on-site erosion and sediment control manager must conduct **weekly inspections** Oct 1 – Apr 30
- Additional inspection required **within 24 hours of any rain event over 0.5 inches**
- Silt fence required at site perimeter to prevent sediment runoff

### Impact on Homeowner Timelines
- Grading **can** be done in winter but adds cost (erosion control materials, inspection time)
- Permits may take longer in wet season as inspections are more complex
- Projects that disturb less than 50 cu yd may avoid permit but still need erosion control if they expose soil
- Ideal grading season: May–September (dry soil, faster permit processing, lower erosion risk)

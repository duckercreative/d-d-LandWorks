## Technical Depth Requirements

### Irrigation Trench Depth (this page's #1 technical asset)

| Line type | Depth (Willamette Valley standard) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Irrigation mainline | 6–8 inches typical | Deeper (12 in) near freeze-risk areas or where equipment will run over |
| Irrigation lateral | 4–6 inches | Minimal depth; protected from shovel and aerator damage |
| Drip / micro tubing | Surface to 2–4 inches | Often under mulch, not fully buried |

**Critical framing:** Oregon has NO state code minimum burial depth for irrigation lines. Unlike water service lines (OPSC 609.1 requires 24 inches in Lane County) or electrical conduit (NEC 300.5), irrigation is governed by standard practice only. State this explicitly — it is the honest answer to a common homeowner question.

**Winterization:** In Lane County, irrigation systems must be drained or blown out before any hard freeze — not buried below frost depth (unlike water service lines). The Willamette Valley frost depth is 12 inches, but the mild Pacific climate means irrigation systems drain for winter rather than being buried that deep.

### Oregon 811 (mandatory for all trenching, including irrigation)

Oregon law (ORS 757.545) requires notification of Oregon 811 before ANY excavation — including irrigation trenching on private property. Call 811 or submit online at oregon811.org at least 2 business days before digging. Ticket valid 30 calendar days.

**This is the differentiator:** zero local Eugene contractors state that OR811 applies to irrigation. Homeowners assume it only applies to utility work. Publishing the accurate legal requirement (with ORS citation) is the highest-authority, lowest-competition move in this content space.

| Scenario | Permit required? |
|---|---|
| Irrigation trench on private property (not crossing ROW) | **No** |
| Drainage ditch on private property (not crossing ROW) | **No** (unless grading >50 CY) |

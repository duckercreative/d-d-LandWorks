## 1. Frost Depth and Minimum Footing Depth — INHERITED + CONFIRMED

**ORSC R403.1.4 — Minimum footing depth (exact code text via UpCodes):**
> "Exterior footings shall be placed not less than **12 inches (305 mm)** below the finished grade on undisturbed ground surface."

**ORSC R403.1.4.1 — Frost Protection:** Footings must extend below the frost line in Table R301.2(1), OR be constructed per ASCE 32 / R403.3 frost-protected shallow foundation, OR on solid rock.

**ORSC Table R301.2(1) — Frost Line by Elevation:**
- Below 2,500 ft: **12 inches** minimum
- 2,500–4,000 ft: 18 inches
- At or above 4,000 ft: 24 inches

**Lane County application:** Eugene sits at approximately 430 ft elevation (well below 2,500 ft). The entire Lane County populated valley floor — Eugene, Springfield, Cottage Grove, Junction City, Creswell, Veneta, Florence — falls in the 12-inch band. The frost protection depth and the minimum depth rule land on the same number: **12 inches**.

**Eugene-specific note from agent research (2026-09-24):** Saturated Willamette Valley clay in wet season may require **over-excavation beyond the 12-inch code minimum** to reach firm bearing soil. A contractor who quotes "12 inches" as the final answer without a site assessment is not accounting for this.

**Source:** UpCodes ORSC 2021/2023 (up.codes); footingdepth.com (ORSC 2023 synthesis); secondary confirmation from manufactured dwelling code Lane County table · Verified 2026-09-09 (base pack); confirmed via agent research 2026-09-24.

## 2. Foundation Drainage — R405 IS Required in Lane County (Key Local Finding)

**ORSC R405 — Foundation Drain:**
- Drainage systems are required around concrete or masonry foundations that retain earth and enclose habitable or usable space (basements, habitable crawlspaces)
- **Exception:** if the foundation sits on well-drained ground or sand-gravel mixture soils (Group I soils, Unified Soil Classification System)

**Lane County/Eugene application:** Malpass clay and Willamette Valley alluvial clay soils are NOT Group I well-drained soils. **The R405 drainage requirement IS triggered for any basement or enclosed crawlspace in the Willamette Valley.** This is an affirmative statement that no local competitor makes — it directly answers "do I need a foundation drain in Oregon" for Eugene homeowners: yes, in most Lane County soil conditions.

**ORSC R401.3 — Grade at Foundation Perimeter:**
> "Grade shall fall not fewer than 6 inches (152 mm) within the first 10 feet (3,048 mm)"
> Impervious surfaces within 10 feet must slope minimum 2% away from the building foundation.

**Source:** codes.iccsafe.org ORSC R405 (secondary reproducing primary text); UpCodes · Verified 2026-09-09 (base pack), confirmed via agent research 2026-09-24.

## 3. OSHA — Over-Dig and Working-Space — INHERITED

Per OSHA 1995 interpretation letter (house foundation/basement carve-out from 1926.652):
- Minimum working width at bottom of excavation: **not less than 2 feet**
- Applies when excavation is under 7.5 ft deep OR properly benched (2 ft horizontal per 5 ft vertical)
- No water, tension cracks, or destabilizing conditions; no heavy equipment vibration near workers; surcharge loads kept back

**ADU/near-existing-structure rule (OSHA 1926.652(b)):** Excavating below the base of an existing footing requires an underpinning support system OR a PE to certify no hazard.

**Source:** OSHA interpretation letter 1995-06-30; OSHA 1926.652 · Verified 2026-09-09.

## 6. Eugene/Lane County Soil Context — KEY LOCAL DIFFERENTIATORS

### Malpass Clay (West Eugene)
**Source: BLM Technical Note 447 — primary federal source**
- Dominant heavy clay horizon in the West Eugene Wetlands region
- Heavy clay horizon forms at approximately **19 inches depth** in some soil profiles
- High shrink/swell properties; very low permeability

**Source: City of Eugene — Land and Water in West Eugene Geology — primary municipal source**
- Confirms clay-dominant geology in west Eugene

**Source: TerraFirma (goterrafirma.com — regional foundation contractor) — secondary/practitioner**
- Eugene homes typically built on crawl space foundations with cripple walls
- Dominant soil: volcanic-derived clay loam and sandy loam
- Clay soils + wet winters = continuous stress on foundation walls and supports
- Helical piers commonly used on homes built on shifting clay and alluvial soils
- Persistent winter saturation + Cascadia seismic risk = primary foundation risk drivers in Eugene

### Willamette Valley Alluvial Clay (Valley Floor Broadly)
**Source: Cojo Asphalt blog (Oregon contractor) — secondary/practitioner**
- Valley floor: deep clay and silt deposited by ancient Missoula Floods
- "Digs easily when dry, but pumps and ruts when wet"
- Saturated valley clay turns soft under load
- Subgrades need compaction and base rock in wet conditions

**Source: Wilsonville geotechnical report (2023) — primary/professional geotechnical (Willamette Valley, applicable regionally)**
- Shallow, perched groundwater and saturated soils should be anticipated in wet-weather excavations
- Dewatering systems: ditches, sumps, and pumps adequate for perched groundwater control
- Native soils are moisture-sensitive: wet-season foundations may require **over-excavation of footings and backfill with compacted structural fill**

### Eugene Valley — No Significant Bedrock
**Source: Research agent synthesis + Eugene geology docs — secondary/confirmed**
- Unlike Central Oregon (basalt rock a major excavation challenge), the Willamette Valley floor has **no significant bedrock**
- Rock is NOT a typical Eugene excavation challenge
- **Clay is the primary challenge in Eugene — not rock**
- This is a Eugene-specific differentiator (many national pages/guides focus on rock as a cost driver; that framing doesn't fit Eugene)

### Foundation Type Prevalence in Eugene
**Source: lanecountyhomes.net blog (ranking realtor blog, Oct 2024) — secondary/local**
- "The three foundation types you will encounter most often in Eugene are **crawl space (the most common), slab-on-grade, and — in older or rural... post and pier**"
- **Crawl space is most common in Eugene** — directly relevant to what the excavation page should lead with in its foundation type section

## 7. Oregon Wet Season — Seasonal Framing

**Source: Cojo Asphalt / regional practitioners — secondary/practitioner**
- Wet season: **October through May** (7-month window in the Willamette Valley)
- Dry window: **May–October** (May–September ideal for excavation scheduling)
- Dewatering method for residential: trash pumps in sumps handle steady seepage; well-point systems for deeper/longer trenches
- Dewatering is continuous during dig, bedding, and initial backfill phases
- Wet clay haul-off is a significant cost line item in wet season

## 9. ADU Foundation Rules (Eugene-Specific)

**Source: Multiple secondary sources (aduplanet.com, cooperdesignbuilders.com, Oregon state ADU guidance) — secondary**
- ADU foundations must comply with **ORSC R403 and R405** (same frost depth, same drainage requirements as primary residence)
- Foundation inspection is a required milestone (same excavate → inspect → pour sequence)
- **OSHA 1926.652(b)** applies when excavating for an ADU footing near or below an existing structure's footing — underpinning required or PE certification of no hazard
- Eugene allows ADUs under ORS 197.312 state law; Eugene has its own ADU overlay on state rules
- Manufactured home ADUs: must be placed on excavated/backfilled foundations of concrete or masonry; foundation must elevate structure no more than 12 inches above finished grade

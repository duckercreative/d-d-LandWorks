### 5–10 Customer Scenarios (Scenario Layer, Phase 5.5)

1. **"We just got approved for an ADU permit in west Eugene — the builder wants excavation done in 3 weeks during November"** — Late fall, Malpass clay area, tight timeline. Key anxieties: Can you actually dig in wet season? How deep do we go? What happens to the soil? Excavation timeline adds pressure to the whole build sequence.

2. **"We're building a new home on a lot in Creswell — the GC said to hire an excavation contractor directly for the foundation dig, then they'll take over"** — First-time home builder, Lane County unincorporated. Anxieties: Who pulls the permit? Who schedules the footing inspection? What's my role as owner?

3. **"We have an older crawl space home in the Whiteaker neighborhood and we're trying to add a basement under part of it — is that even possible?"** — Urban Eugene, under-existing-structure excavation. Anxieties: Is it safe? How much will it cost? Will it destabilize the existing structure? (OSHA 1926.652(b) underpinning rule applies directly.)

4. **"We're building a detached shop on our rural property east of Springfield — slab or crawl space?"** — Rural Lane County, east of city, drier site than west Eugene. Different soil than Malpass clay; decision between slab and crawl space has real cost and drainage implications.

5. **"We got a bid that's $3,000 higher than we expected — the contractor said it's because of 'soil conditions'"** — Eugene clay soil cost surprise. They've already seen one bid and are researching to understand if the explanation is legitimate. They need an authoritative source that explains Willamette Valley clay behavior.

6. **"We're planning a slab foundation for a modular home — what does excavation actually include?"** — Slab-specific; Willamette Valley slab-on-grade is straightforward but still requires topsoil strip, compaction, and inspection. They think excavation is only for basements.

7. **"I'm a general contractor — my excavation sub just canceled — I need foundation work done in 10 days in Springfield"** — GC, not homeowner. Reliability, coordination, CCB credentials, and speed are the decision factors, not educational content.

8. **"My neighbor mentioned something about calling 811 — do I need to worry about that for a foundation dig?"** — Oregon811 / utility-locate concern from a homeowner who overheard it but doesn't know what it means or who's responsible. Answer: contractor is responsible; D&D calls on every job.

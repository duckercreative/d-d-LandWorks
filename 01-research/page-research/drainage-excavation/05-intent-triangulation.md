## Table 3 — Trust Killers and Rebuttals

| Trust killer | Rebuttal to build into the page |
|---|---|
| A contractor who can't or won't explain where the water actually goes | State the three real destinations plainly — daylight, dry well, or a permitted storm-drain tie-in — and that a drain with none of these just relocates the problem |
| Vague "we do drainage" language with no depth, material, or slope specifics | State the real, sourced depth (18-24 in. standard), slope (~1% grade), pipe-material distinction (corrugated vs. PVC), and gravel/fabric detail |
| No mention that a storm-drain tie-in requires a permit and, in Eugene, is restricted to specific permit-holders | State the real EC §6.610 rule plainly, including who can pull the permit |
| Silence on whether D&D actually installs French drains | Do not overclaim — state the open item honestly if it remains unconfirmed at publish time, exactly as the grading-leveling and foundation-excavation pages already do |
| Confusing a French drain with the code-required foundation drain, or with a septic drainfield | One clear disambiguation section for each, cross-linked rather than re-explained |
| No acknowledgment that a previous regrading attempt might have been the wrong fix | Address it directly and honestly — some water problems need underground conveyance, not surface reshaping, and that's not a sign the earlier work was done badly |
| Zero reviews / zero online presence at launch | Do not fabricate — same rule as every prior pack |

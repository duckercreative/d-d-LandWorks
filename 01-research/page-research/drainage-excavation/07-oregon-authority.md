## 1. Storm-Drain Tie-In Permitting — Eugene (VERIFIED, the single most load-bearing new fact for this page)

- **Governing code:** Eugene Code §6.610 ("Stormwater and Sewers – Connection – Equivalent Assessments").
- **The rule, stated plainly:** No person may connect a drain, downspout, or other conduit carrying stormwater to the city's stormwater system without first paying the required fees and obtaining a permit from the City Engineer.
- **Who can even pull the permit — a real, specific, checkable restriction:** Permits are issued only to (a) a property owner, for a connection serving their own personal residence, or (b) a licensed plumber or a licensed septic-tank-installation contractor. This is a genuinely uncommon level of specificity to find and cite — it directly answers "who is allowed to do this work" rather than just "is a permit required."
- **Issuance standard:** The City Engineer issues the permit upon payment of the required fee/assessments and a determination that the proposed connection is a proper use of the city stormwater system — i.e., not an automatic rubber stamp.
- **Maintenance responsibility after connection:** Lines connecting private catch basins or pipe segments into the city storm system are *not* a city maintenance responsibility once connected, though material from a private system can migrate into and affect a public catch basin during city cleaning — a real, honest caveat worth including rather than implying the city takes over maintenance.
- **Why this matters for the page:** Zero of the 5 competitors examined in this pack's drainage-specific pass (Danco, All Dirt, Heavy D, M4 Landworks, Pacific Earthworks) mention this permit, this code section, or the who-can-pull-it restriction anywhere. This is the single most concrete, specific, easily-verified Oregon authority signal available to this page — see `08-gap-analysis.md` Gap #1.
- **Source:** [Eugene Code §6.610 — Stormwater and Sewers, Connection](https://eugene.municipal.codes/EC/6.610) (direct fetch returned 403 this session; content captured via search-engine summary of the same code text — flag for a direct-fetch re-verification pass before publishing the exact fee/assessment mechanics as litigation-proof, though the core permit-required/who-can-pull-it facts are corroborated clearly enough to cite with confidence) · [City of Eugene — Stormwater Management](https://www.eugene-or.gov/463/Stormwater) · verified 2026-09-09.

## 2. Storm-Drain Tie-In Permitting — Springfield (VERIFIED, less granular than Eugene's)

- Connecting to Springfield's public stormwater system, or discharging to a waterway, generally requires a permit and must meet local stormwater standards.
- Springfield's Development Code was amended by **City Council Ordinance 6464**, adopted November 6, 2023, effective January 8, 2024, updating stormwater post-construction requirements.
- Residential project submittals must show how stormwater and wastewater connect — to the public system, a septic system, or a dry well — per Springfield's own residential submittal checklist.
- Springfield's stormwater program operates under DEQ's Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4) permit, a federal Clean Water Act framework.
- **No single, quotable code section as specific as Eugene's EC §6.610 was found in this pass** for Springfield — present the rule generally, and recommend confirming exact permit/fee mechanics directly with Springfield's Development & Public Works Department before quoting an exact process to a customer.
- **Source:** [City of Springfield — Stormwater](https://springfield-or.gov/city/development-public-works/clean-water-and-stormwater/) · [Springfield Residential Submittal Checklist](https://www.springfield-or.gov/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/ResidentialSubmittalChecklist.pdf) · [Springfield Development Code stormwater amendments](https://springfield-or.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Amendments-to-the-Springfield-Development-Code-Stormwater-Post-Construction.pdf) · verified 2026-09-09.

## 4. DEQ 1200-C Construction Stormwater Permit (reused, applied contextually)

- Reused from `../site-preparation/07-oregon-authority.md` — triggered by construction activity (clearing, grading, excavation) disturbing **1 acre or more**.
- Applied honestly here: most residential yard-drainage and even most residential French-drain jobs fall far under this threshold. Mention for scale/context only — do not imply a typical homeowner drainage job needs this permit.
- A discharge into a ditch or open drain that ultimately feeds a named waterway is treated, for DEQ purposes, as a discharge to that waterway (a ditch is a human-made conveyance, not an exemption) — relevant context if a project's scale ever does cross the 1-acre threshold.
- **Source (reused):** [Oregon DEQ — 1200 Series Construction Stormwater Permits](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/wq/wqpermits/pages/stormwater-construction.aspx) · verified in the site-preparation pack, re-confirmed still current 2026-09-09.

## 5. Oregon DSL Removal-Fill Law (reused from the excavation pack's pond-permitting research)

- Reused verbatim from `../excavation/07-oregon-authority.md` §4: Oregon's Department of State Lands (DSL) requires a Removal-Fill permit for removing or filling **50 cubic yards or more** of material in "waters of the state," including wetlands, streams, and riparian zones — and even smaller volumes can require a permit if there's a significant environmental impact.
- **Applied to this page's context:** most drainage discharge points (daylighting onto a lawn, into a dry well, or into a permitted storm-system tie-in) do not touch DSL-jurisdictional water at all. DSL relevance arises specifically if a drain's discharge point is a mapped wetland, stream, or other jurisdictional water — a real but narrower scenario than most yard-drainage jobs.

## 6. Oregon811 and OSHA Trench Safety (reused verbatim, unchanged)

Fully reused from `../excavation/07-oregon-authority.md` §§1-2 — not re-verified:

- Oregon811 (Oregon Utility Notification Center): free, at least 2 business days' advance notice before any digging project, including a drainage trench.
- OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P: trenches 5 ft deep or more require a protective system; safe access required for excavations 4 ft deep or more. Most yard-drainage trenches (18-36 in.) fall well under these thresholds; a French drain tied to a footing (4-6 ft) can approach or cross them — worth noting as a genuine depth-dependent safety distinction specific to this page's deeper-application content.

## 7. French Drain / Curtain Drain Technical Specifications (new — sourced from national cost-guide and Oregon-specific contractor content)

- **Standard yard-drainage depth:** 18-24 inches; shallow surface-water-only applications can run as shallow as 8-12 inches. A French drain tied to a footing runs to the footing's own depth (commonly 4-6 ft).
- **Trench width:** 6-12 inches for residential applications.
- **Slope:** approximately 1% grade — roughly 1 inch of drop every 8-10 feet — to keep water moving rather than sitting in the pipe.
- **Pipe material:** rigid perforated PVC is more durable and resists crushing/collapse better than flimsy corrugated pipe, though corrugated costs less and is more flexible around obstacles; installed holes-down so water enters at the gravel bed rather than needing to rise within the pipe.
- **Backfill:** clean, angular washed gravel (commonly 3/4-inch), wrapped in filter fabric to prevent Willamette Valley's silty clay from migrating into and clogging the system over time.
- **Cost (national ranges, not Lane-County-specific):** $10-$100+ per linear foot depending on depth and type; shallow exterior drains run $10-$35/ft, deep footing-level drains run $45-$85/ft; curtain drains (shallower, ~2 ft) run $10-$25/ft. Adverse conditions (clay slumping, winter water table, rock) can push actual costs 2-3x these baselines per Oregon-specific contractor content.
- **Dry well cost (national):** roughly $1,165-$4,420 installed, averaging around $3,030-$3,110; hand-dug pits run $5-$13/linear ft for labor plus $45-$55/yard for gravel fill.
- **Catch basin cost (national):** $1,000-$4,000 installed; plastic catch basins alone run $20-$130 for materials.
- **Underground downspout extension (national):** $150-$350 per downspout, including catch basin, extension, trenching, and pipe.
- **Source:** [HomeGuide — French Drain Cost](https://homeguide.com/costs/french-drain-cost) · [Angi — French Drain Cost](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-french-drain-cost.htm) · [Hunker — French Drain vs. Curtain Drain](https://www.hunker.com/13425283/french-drain-vs-curtain-drain/) · [Cojo Asphalt — French Drain Trench Excavation in Oregon](https://www.cojoasphalt.com/blog/french-drain-trench-excavation-oregon) · [LawnLove — Dry Well Installation Cost](https://lawnlove.com/blog/dry-well-installation-cost/) · [HomeGuide — Drainage System Installation Cost](https://homeguide.com/costs/drainage-system-installation-cost) · all national/industry sources, none Lane-County-exact; verified 2026-09-09.

## Top 5 gaps to prioritize (achievable within a first content pass)

1. **Standalone-service naming** (score 50.0) — the structural finding underlying everything else: simply presenting grading/leveling as its own real service for an existing yard, which zero competitors do
2. **"Landscaping job vs. excavation job" disambiguation** (score 50.0, tied) — the task brief's own named central content-gap opportunity for this page
3. **Positive-drainage-slope / code-standard explanation** (score 50.0, tied) — this page's single most citable, most differentiating passage, backed by a directly-verified Oregon code citation
4. **Permit-simplicity reassurance**, **topdressing-vs-regrade honesty**, **small-job reassurance**, and **foundation-damage-risk education** (all 40.0, four-way tie) — all zero-cost, all directly answer real, sourced homeowner pain points from `05-intent-triangulation.md`
5. **Swale vs. French drain education** and **seasonal-timing scaled to job size** (30.0 each) — genuine, low-cost trust-building content unique to this page

## The single biggest content-gap opportunity (headline finding)

**Zero of the 8 competitor sites re-checked for this page — including All Dirt, directly fetched and confirmed — offer, name, or explain standalone yard leveling/regrading as a service distinct from grading tied to a new dig or new construction.** Every mention of "grading" anywhere in the 8-competitor set sits inside a general excavation or site-prep service list, always framed around foundations, subdivisions, or new-construction pads. This is a structurally different, and arguably larger, opportunity than the FAQ/pricing/permit-content gaps found on the three prior pages: it isn't that competitors answer this page's questions poorly, it's that none of them have identified this as a distinct buyer need at all. Independently corroborated by HomeAdvisor's own lead-generation taxonomy, which already splits "Landscape Minor Grading or Resloping" from "Excavation Major Grading or Resloping" as separate categories — the market recognizes this buyer exists; no Lane County excavation contractor's own content currently speaks to them directly.

## How this page draws its line against the Excavation & Grading and Site Preparation pages (per the topical map's cannibalization note, §16, and the shipped excavation content)

The shipped `/excavation/` page's own H2 ("What's the Difference Between Excavation and Grading?") already states the boundary this page must respect verbatim: *"This page covers grading that happens because something is being dug: cut-and-fill to design grade, backfill and compaction after a trench or footing goes in, and rough grading that finishes an excavation project. Standalone re-sloping, yard leveling, or drainage-grading on ground where nothing new is being dug is a different job, covered on the grading and leveling page instead."* This pack's job was to make sure the Grading & Leveling page actually is that "different job" — which it is: standalone re-sloping, yard leveling, and drainage-grading for an **existing** surface problem, no new dig involved. Against Site Preparation, the line is separate and equally clean: site prep grades a **raw, undeveloped lot** ahead of new construction; this page grades an **already-developed lot or yard** with a surface problem, independent of any new build. Neither boundary required inventing a new rule — both were already established by the sibling pages' own shipped content and the topical map's cannibalization table, and this pack's job was to confirm and apply them, not redraw them.

**One current-live-page issue worth flagging for the content brief:** the live `/grading-leveling/` page (`site/src/pages/services/grading-leveling.astro`) currently lists "Cutting building pads level and to design elevation" as its first bullet under "Slope Is the Whole Job." Per the scope boundary confirmed in this pack (and the shipped excavation page's own language), building-pad grading to design elevation ahead of a build is Excavation & Grading / Site Preparation territory, not this page's — it is grading that happens *because* something is about to be built or dug, the exact case the excavation page's boundary language excludes from this page. This should be reviewed and likely removed or reframed during the content brief, not carried forward as-is.

## What the current live page (`site/src/pages/services/grading-leveling.astro`) already gets right

- Frames the core concept correctly and vividly: "Grading isn't just flattening dirt. It's setting a slope shallow enough to be usable but steep enough that water actually moves off it" — this is directionally exactly right and pairs well with the ORSC §R401.3 figure this pack sourced to make it precise rather than just evocative
- Already lists genuinely on-scope bullet items: grading yards/lawns for positive drainage, correcting low spots, shaping berms and swales, fine grading ahead of landscaping/hardscape
- Already names equipment generically and accurately (dozer/skid steer for bulk shaping, excavator with a smooth bucket and laser level for fine grading) without fabricating brand names
- Already states CCB #261742 and links to the service-area page
- Does **not** currently mention the drainage-slope code standard, permit guidance, the landscaping-vs-excavation disambiguation, FAQ content, topdressing-vs-regrade guidance, or the swale-vs-French-drain distinction — these are exactly the gaps this file identifies as highest-priority to add
- **Does** currently include one item (building-pad grading to design elevation) that falls outside this page's confirmed scope boundary and should be revisited — see above

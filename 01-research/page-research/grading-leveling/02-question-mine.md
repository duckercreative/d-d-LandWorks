## oregon_specific

36. What is a "positive drainage slope," exactly? *(directly explains this page's central technical concept — zero competitors currently answer this anywhere)*
37. How much slope does a yard actually need for water to drain properly? *(answered precisely: Oregon's own adopted residential code, ORSC §R401.3 — verified directly against ICC's published code text 2026-09-08 — requires a minimum 6-inch fall over the first 10 feet from a foundation (about a 5% slope), tapering to a 2% minimum slope for impervious surfaces within that same 10 feet and generally continuing at 2% or better beyond it)*
38. Do I need a permit just to regrade my lawn in Lane County? *(answered: generally no for an ordinary small yard-regrade — Lane County's Grading and Fill Permit is triggered by moving more than 50 cubic yards of earth or creating a cut/fill slope steeper than 2:1, a threshold most yard-sized jobs stay well under; a large, deep full-property regrade could approach it, so the honest answer is "usually not, but confirm for a big job" rather than a blanket no)*
39. How much land can I grade in Oregon before I need a permit? *(same >50 cu yd / 2:1 slope threshold, applied generally)*
40. Is there a best time of year to regrade a yard in the Willamette Valley's rainy climate? *(the same May-October dry-season logic established in the homepage pack applies here — clay-heavy Willamette Valley soil compacts and grades poorly when saturated, so the dry window is preferred, though a small yard job is far more schedule-flexible than a full excavation project)*
41. Can a yard actually be regraded during Oregon's rainy season, or does it have to wait? *(seasonal/objection-adjacent — workable but slower and messier in wet clay, consistent with the homepage pack's clay-soil findings)*

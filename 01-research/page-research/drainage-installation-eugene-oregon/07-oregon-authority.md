### 1. Eugene Stormwater Connection Permit — EC §6.610

The parent pack's single most load-bearing authority signal. Reproduced here for the brief author's convenience — **do not re-derive; cite the parent pack's research:**

- **Governing code:** Eugene Code §6.610 (Stormwater and Sewers – Connection – Equivalent Assessments)
- **Core rule:** No person may connect a drain, downspout, or other conduit carrying stormwater to the city's stormwater system without paying the required fees and obtaining a permit from the City Engineer.
- **Who can pull the permit:** Only (a) a property owner, for a connection serving their own personal residence, or (b) a licensed plumber or a licensed septic-tank-installation contractor.
- **Issuance standard:** City Engineer issues upon payment and determination the connection is a proper use of the city storm system — not an automatic rubber stamp.
- **Maintenance after connection:** The private line and any catch basins feeding the public system remain the property owner's maintenance responsibility after connection.
- **Source:** Eugene Code §6.610 (direct fetch returned 403 in the parent research session; facts corroborated via search-engine summary — flag for direct-fetch re-verification of current fee amounts before quoting specific dollar figures to a customer). ### 2. Springfield Stormwater Connection Requirements — Ordinance 6464

- Springfield's Development Code amended by City Council Ordinance 6464, adopted November 6, 2023, effective January 8, 2024.
- Connecting to Springfield's public storm system or discharging to a waterway generally requires a permit and must meet local stormwater standards.
- Residential submittals must show how stormwater and wastewater connect — to the public system, a septic system, or a dry well.
- Less granular public detail found than Eugene's EC §6.610 — present as a general rule; direct customers to Springfield Development & Public Works for exact permit process.
- **Source:** Springfield Development Code stormwater amendments, January 2024. Verified 2026-09-09. **Re-check if content build is more than 90 days after that date.**

### 5. DEQ 1200-C (Construction Stormwater) and Oregon DSL

- **DEQ 1200-C:** Triggered by construction disturbance of 1+ acre. Most yard-drainage jobs fall far under this threshold. Mention for scale/context only.
- **Oregon DSL Removal-Fill Law:** Relevant only if drain discharge point is a mapped wetland/stream/jurisdictional water. Most daylight/dry-well/storm-system discharges do not trigger it. Cross-link to `/excavation/` for depth.
- **Source:** Both reused from `../excavation/07-oregon-authority.md`. Verified 2026-09-09.

### 6. Oregon 811 and OSHA Trench Safety

- Oregon 811 (Dial 811): free, at least 2 business days before any digging, including a drainage trench.
- OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P: trenches 5 ft deep or more require a protective system. Most yard-drainage trenches (18–36 in.) fall well under this threshold. A French drain tied to a footing (4–6 ft) can approach or cross it.
- **Source:** Reused from `../excavation/07-oregon-authority.md`. Not re-verified.

### 8. Malpass Clay — Eugene Soil Context

Reused from `../foundation-excavation-eugene-oregon/07-oregon-authority.md` §Soil, applied here to drainage (not foundation) context:

- **Malpass clay** is the dominant soil series in west Eugene. Documented in BLM Technical Note 447.
- **Key properties relevant to drainage:** high shrink-swell, low permeability, poor natural drainage. Water does not percolate readily through Malpass clay — it sits on top of or moves laterally through saturated zones. This is precisely why buried drainage systems (rather than just surface regrading) are often necessary in west Eugene.
- **Drainage implication:** Filter fabric is non-negotiable in Malpass clay. Without geotextile wrapping, fine clay particles migrate into the gravel and pipe over time, clogging the system within a few years. A properly built French drain in Eugene clay includes filter fabric; a cheap/shortcuts build does not — and fails sooner.
- **Cost implication:** Willamette Valley clay can add 2–3× to the standard national baseline for drainage installation, per Cojo Asphalt's Oregon-specific contractor notes. Clay slumping, wet-season scheduling, and dewatering all affect real project costs.
- **Source:** BLM Technical Note 447 (Malpass clay); Cojo Asphalt Oregon French drain notes (clay cost multiplier). Both verified in parent packs.

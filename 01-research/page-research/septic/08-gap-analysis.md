## Top 5 gaps to prioritize (by score, achievable within a first content pass)

1. **Distressed-buyer reassurance** and **repair-vs-replace decision guidance** (tied, score 50.0 each) — the two highest-leverage, zero-cost content decisions on this page, and the ones most directly tied to the task's own framing of septic as a health/safety-critical, often-urgent service
2. **Home-sale septic-inspection clarity** (score 40.0) — resolves a genuinely confusing, high-anxiety question with a factual, sourced answer
3. **License-verification link to DEQ's business search tool** (score 40.0) — a single hyperlink, same pattern as the homepage's CCB link
4. **Trust-killer/anti-exploitation messaging around crisis pricing** (score 40.0) — directly serves the task's explicit concern that a distressed buyer needs reassurance, not upsell pressure
5. **DEQ-vs-Lane-County regulatory clarity** and **Willamette Valley soil/water-table framing** (tied, score 30.0 each) — both are zero-cost, Oregon-specific, and answer the exact regulatory questions the task brief asked this pack to verify

## The single biggest content-gap opportunity overall

**The conventional-vs-alternative system cost explanation (Gap #1) combined with the repair-vs-replace decision framework (Gap #3).** Every competitor in both competitive sets (septic-capable excavators and dedicated specialists) hides behind "call for a free estimate" on what is, for most homeowners, one of the single most expensive and least-understood line items they'll ever face on their property. A page that plainly explains *why* one system costs 3-4x another, and *how* to think about repairing versus replacing a failing system, has zero direct competition anywhere in the researched SERP — a materially bigger whitespace than anything found on the four prior pages in this pipeline, where at least some cost/process content existed somewhere in the competitive set.

## Gaps that require David's input before they can be filled (do not fabricate)

- Exact DEQ credential type/number David holds (Installer Certification vs. Combined license vs. Maintenance Provider — see `07-oregon-authority.md` §2)
- Years of septic-specific experience (distinct from general excavation experience)
- Number of septic systems installed
- Which specific system types D&D actually installs (conventional only, or also specific ATT brands/types like AdvanTex or a particular sand-filter product)
- Whether D&D's DEQ standing covers Maintenance Provider certification (relevant only if D&D services ATT/sand-filter systems on an ongoing basis, not just installs them)
- Real response-time capability for an "emergency" septic call, before using any urgency-based service language
- Any real customer testimonials or before/after project photos

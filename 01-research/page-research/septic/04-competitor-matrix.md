# Competitor Matrix — Septic Install & Repair (`/services/septic-install-repairs/`)

**Headline finding, confirmed directly this session:** the competitive set for septic is genuinely different from the excavation/grading/land-clearing competitive set this project has used for the prior five pages. Of the 8 excavation competitors originally deep-dived in the homepage pack, **only 4 (half) offer septic installation/repair themselves**, one explicitly does NOT and instead supports other septic installers, and 4 dedicated septic/sewer specialty companies were found competing in this exact keyword space who never appeared anywhere in the excavation, grading, or land-clearing research. This matters for the brief: D&D's real competition on this page is not the same 8-10 names used everywhere else — it is a smaller, split field of septic-capable excavators plus a genuinely separate specialist category.

Against the other 4 (Danco, Heavy D, Strasheim, A&D-unconfirmed), septic capability alone is already a differentiator, independent of any content or positioning decision.

## Individual Notes Worth Carrying Into the Brief

**Danco Excavation's site-prep-for-septic-contractors positioning** is the single most useful competitor finding for framing D&D's own message: it demonstrates that even a company with a real DEQ number sometimes chooses to specialize narrowly rather than claim the full install. D&D's actual full-service claim (site prep through septic install, under one contractor) is a genuinely stronger positioning than Danco's split model — worth stating directly, without naming Danco, as "D&D doesn't just prepare the site for someone else's septic install — the certification and the crew are the same company."

**All Dirt's "specialized drainfields (pressure-dosed and mound systems)" language** is the most technically specific septic copy found among excavation-generalist competitors — a genuine benchmark to match or exceed on system-type naming, once D&D's actual installed system types are confirmed.

**Braun naming "advantex, sand filters, and city sewers" by name** shows that naming specific ATT products/brands is realistic content for this niche (a homeowner searching "advantex septic system oregon" is a real, if smaller, decision-stage query — see `01-keyword-universe.csv`). Do not copy Braun's specific brand claims for D&D without confirming what D&D actually installs.

**Oregon Septic, Best Septic, and Westco's pumping/inspection-first positioning** confirms that the "maintenance relationship" side of the septic market (recurring pumping, camera inspections) is being captured by specialists who don't do installation at all — reinforcing that D&D's install/repair-only scope is a coherent, defensible niche rather than an incomplete offering.

## 1. Oregon811 / Call-Before-You-Dig — VERIFIED DIRECTLY (new, and the single most important fact for this page)

Per the task's own instruction to verify this rather than assume it, this was fetched directly from `digsafelyoregon.com` and `oregon.gov/puc`, not inferred:

- **Official name:** The **Oregon Utility Notification Center (OUNC)**, operating publicly under the brand name "**Oregon 811**." This is a real, currently-operating one-call center, not a generic reference to the national 811 number.
- **Is it free?** Yes — digsafelyoregon.com states locate requests are free ("Oregon 811: Dig Safely with Free Locate Requests").
- **Advance notice required:** **At least 2 business days** before starting any digging project, per oregon.gov/puc's own stated language: "Call 811 or toll free 1-800-332-2344 at least two business days before starting any digging project." Holidays (e.g., Labor Day) do not count toward this notice period.
- **What happens after the call:** The One-Call Center notifies affected utility operators, who then have 2 business days to locate and mark their underground facilities with color-coded paint.
- **A separate, distinct figure — do not conflate:** A 2026 amendment to Oregon Administrative Rule (OAR) 952-001-0010(23) changed the *locate ticket's validity period* ("ticket life") from 45 days to 30 days. This is how long a completed locate stays valid for the digger to act on — it is **not** the advance-notice requirement, which remains 2 business days. Content should keep these two figures clearly separate.
- **Who must call:** digsafelyoregon.com addresses both "Homeowners" and "Excavators" as distinct audiences, and Oregon's excavation laws apply to digging by contractors and property owners alike — both must call before digging on private property or in a public right-of-way.
- **Penalties for not calling:** The Oregon Public Utility Commission (PUC) enforces Oregon's dig laws and can take enforcement action against excavators who skip the one-call system. **No specific penalty dollar amount was found in this research pass** — this project's no-fabrication rule means content should state the PUC's enforcement role plainly without inventing a fine amount, and can recommend contacting the PUC directly (503-378-6600) for exact penalty exposure if a customer asks.
- **Why this matters for the page:** Zero of the 10 competitor sites examined across this pack and the two prior packs mention Oregon811, utility locates, or call-before-you-dig anywhere in their content. This is the single most concrete, easily-verified, zero-cost trust/competence signal available to this page — see `08-gap-analysis.md` Gap #1.
- **Source:** [Oregon Utility Notification Center / Oregon811 — Dig Safely](https://digsafelyoregon.com/) · [Oregon PUC — Call Before You Dig](https://www.oregon.gov/puc/safety/pages/call-before-you-dig.aspx) · verified 2026-09-08.

## 2. OSHA Trench & Excavation Safety (reused verbatim, unchanged)

Fully reused from the homepage pack (`../home/07-oregon-authority.md` §4) — no new verification needed, applies identically here:

- Governing standard: 29 CFR 1926, Subpart P ("Excavations"), enforced in Oregon through Oregon OSHA.
- Trenches 5 feet deep or more require a protective system (shoring, shielding, or sloping) unless excavated entirely in stable rock.
- Safe access (ladder, steps, or ramp) is required within 25 feet of lateral travel for any excavation 4 feet deep or more.
- A "competent person" (an OSHA term of art) must be involved in protective-system decisions for excavations under 5 feet.
- Zero of the 10 competitors examined in this pack (or the 8 in the homepage pack) mention trench safety standards anywhere — reusing this citation here, on the excavation page specifically, is a genuine, differentiating authority signal directly relevant to this page's utility-run and foundation-dig project types.
- **Source (reused):** [OSHA — 1926 Subpart P (Excavations)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926SubpartP) · verified 2026-09-08 (original pass).

## 4. Pond Excavation Permitting — Oregon Water Resources Department & Department of State Lands (new)

Genuinely new for this page, since neither the homepage nor site-preparation packs researched the pond project type in any depth:

- **Oregon Water Resources Department (OWRD)** requires a water-rights permit when a pond diverts water from a flowing stream, intermittent waterway, or spring, or when storage exceeds **9.2 acre-feet**.
- **Oregon Department of State Lands (DSL)** regulates work affecting wetlands, streams, riparian zones, or salmon-bearing waterways — relevant if a proposed pond site is near any of these.
- **Generally exempt:** a small, rain-fed pond collecting only rainfall and surface runoff, staying under the 9.2 acre-foot threshold, and positioned 100+ feet from a mapped waterway or wetland.
- **No Lane-County-specific pond ordinance was found** in this research pass — county planning/zoning rules can still apply on top of the state-level framework, and this should be presented as a general structure with a recommendation to confirm with OWRD/DSL/Lane County for a specific parcel, not as an exhaustive checklist.
- **Why this matters for the page:** Pond excavation is named directly in the topical map §6.2 and the task brief as a project type, and only 2 of 10 competitors (McKenzie, BBC Excavation) even mention pond work — neither explains permitting. This is a genuinely uncontested, Oregon-specific content opportunity.
- **Source:** [Iron & Earth Site Services — Pond Construction in Oregon: What You Can Build Without a Permit (and What You Can't)](https://iron-and-earth.com/blog/pond-construction-oregon-permits.html) · verified 2026-09-08. **Caveat:** this is a single contractor-blog source, not a direct fetch of oregon.gov/owrd or oregon.gov/dsl's own permit pages — flag for direct confirmation against OWRD/DSL primary sources before publishing the 9.2 acre-foot figure as an exact, litigation-proof number, consistent with this project's no-fabrication rule.

## 5. Oregon Excavation Cost & Labor Data (new — BLS-sourced)

- Oregon's construction labor costs run roughly **20% above the national average**, per U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics OEWS 2025 data (Operating Engineers & Other Construction Equipment Operators: mean $38.11/hr, $79,270/yr annually in Oregon vs. $66,290/yr nationally). Construction Laborers: $26.79/hr; First-Line Supervisors: $51.13/hr.
- Approximately 5,630 operating engineers, 12,620 construction laborers, and 7,890 construction supervisors are employed statewide in Oregon per this dataset.
- **No single authoritative Lane-County-specific per-cubic-yard excavation price was found** in this research pass — national ranges ($2.50-$15.00/cu yd standard soil, $15-$25/cu yd clay, $50-$200/cu yd rock) should be presented as general, with the Oregon labor-cost premium noted as a reason Lane County pricing likely sits toward the higher end of national ranges rather than stating a fabricated Oregon-specific dollar figure.
- **Source:** [CostFlowAI — Oregon Excavation Cost Calculator](https://costflowai.com/calculators/excavation/oregon/) (cites BLS OEWS 2025 series IDs directly, last verified by the source itself on 2026-06-20) · verified 2026-09-08.

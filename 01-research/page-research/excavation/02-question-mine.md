## project_types

8. What counts as a "general excavation" job versus a specialized one? *(scope-clarity)*
9. Can an excavation contractor dig a pond, or is that a specialty service? *(answered: yes — pond excavation is a named, standard excavation-contractor service per McKenzie and BBC Excavation's own service lists in `04-competitor-matrix.md`)*
10. Do I need a permit to dig a pond in Oregon? *(oregon_specific — answered: Oregon Water Resources Department (OWRD) requires a water-rights permit if the pond diverts from a stream/spring or stores more than 9.2 acre-feet; Department of State Lands (DSL) regulates work near wetlands/salmon-bearing waterways; a small rain-fed pond under the 9.2 acre-foot threshold and 100+ feet from a waterway is generally exempt — Iron & Earth Site Services, verified against OWRD/DSL framing)*
11. How much does it cost to dig a pond? *(answered: farm ponds commonly run $5,000-$25,000 per acre of water; a small 1/4-acre decorative pond runs $4,000-$8,000; a fully engineered 1-acre pond with dam/spillway can start around $45,000 — Bulletproof Pond & Lake / HomeGuide data)*
12. Can an excavation contractor cut a driveway or access road into an undeveloped lot? *(answered: yes, per All Dirt's "site access roads" and Tectonic's "Driveway & Road Excavation" listed service categories)*
13. How much does it cost to excavate for a new driveway? *(answered: $4-$20+/sq ft depending on depth and haul-off in Oregon; a basic 500ft gravel driveway on easy terrain runs $8,000-$25,000+, rising to $15,000-$55,000+ with a culvert and cut/fill on rolling terrain — Cojo Asphalt data)*
14. What's involved in excavating for a utility line (water, sewer, electrical)? *(answered: trenching to the required depth, often with backfill/compaction after pipe or conduit is laid — see topical map §6.7 for the sibling utility-excavation page's scope fence)*
15. How much does utility trenching cost per foot? *(answered: $5-$12/linear ft standard; water lines $15-$25/ft; sewer lines (deeper) $25-$40/ft; electrical $10-$18/ft; difficult conditions (rock, depth, restricted access) push this to $13-$40/ft — Angi/HomeGuide/Airtasker data)*
16. What's the difference between excavating for a foundation versus a basement? *(scope-clarity)*
17. How deep does a foundation or basement excavation need to be? *(answered: standard residential basements run 8-10ft deep; standard footing depth for frost protection is 4-5ft, easily reached by a mini excavator)*
18. How deep can an excavator actually dig? *(answered: mini excavators (1-5 ton class) typically reach 7-12ft depending on size, with some extendable-dipper models reaching 14-16ft)*

## cost

19. How much does excavation cost in general? *(answered nationally: average project $1,400-$6,000, typical $2,200; per cubic yard $2.50-$15.00 for standard soil, $15-$25/cu yd for clay soil specifically)*
20. How much does excavation cost per cubic yard in Oregon specifically? *(oregon_specific — no single authoritative Lane-County-specific per-cubic-yard figure was found in this pass; Oregon's construction labor cost runs roughly 20% above the national average per BLS OEWS 2025 data via CostFlowAI, which supports pricing toward the higher end of national ranges rather than a distinct Oregon-only figure — present as a range, not a fabricated precise number)*
21. How much does it cost to hire an excavator with an operator, per hour? *(answered: $70-$110+/hr for a mini excavator with operator; $120-$150/hr medium excavator; $200-$300+/hr large excavator; an operator alone without equipment runs $50-$150/hr)*
22. Do excavation contractors charge by the hour, the cubic yard, or the job? *(answered: all three models exist — hourly for small/uncertain-scope jobs, per-cubic-yard for larger earthmoving, flat-rate for well-defined projects; reused theme from homepage pack question #16)*
23. What does it cost if the crew hits rock partway through the job? *(objection/anxiety — answered: standard excavation runs $5-$15/cu yd; rock excavation runs $50-$200/cu yd to remove and store on-site, or $220-$250/cu yd hauling off-site — a 10-40x increase; large-volume rock (1,000+ cu yd) may shift from hydraulic hammering to permitted drill-and-blast, which runs $55-$150+/cu yd depending on proximity to structures)*
24. Is ripping rock cheaper than blasting it? *(answered: yes — ripping typically costs 50-65% less than blasting, but only works on soft-to-moderately-firm, fractured rock; blasting is needed for harder rock or larger volumes)*
25. Why do excavation quotes vary so much between contractors for what looks like the same job? *(objection-adjacent — reused root-cause theme from homepage/site-prep packs: soil type, access, season, and depth are the real cost drivers, not contractor markup)*
26. Are there hidden costs in an excavation quote — permits, hauling, dewatering? *(objection, reused from homepage pack question #17)*
27. How much does foundation or basement excavation cost specifically? *(answered: $2,500-$7,300 typical basement/foundation dig; $3,900-$6,800 for a roughly 2.5-day dig per homeblue.com data reused from the homepage pack)*

## process

28. What's the actual sequence: does grading happen before or after excavation, or the reverse? *(ties directly to the excavation-vs-grading disambiguation — answered generally: excavation often happens first to create space below the surface, followed by grading to finalize the surface, though on many jobs the two happen as one continuous earthmoving operation)*
29. What is "cut and fill" and why does it matter? *(answered: redistributing soil on a site — cutting material from high points and using it as fill in low points — to reach a design grade while minimizing hauling cost; a genuinely technical process entity validated by All Dirt's own "What's Included" list)*
30. What is backfill and compaction, and why can't the crew just dump dirt back in a trench? *(answered: backfill must be compacted in layers/lifts so the soil holds weight reliably once something is built or paved over it — uncompacted backfill settles unevenly over time)*
31. What does "site access road" work involve? *(All Dirt-validated inclusion — cutting a stable, usable road/driveway into a lot that doesn't have developed access yet)*
32. What's involved in erosion control during an excavation project? *(All Dirt-validated inclusion — ties to the DEQ 1200-C stormwater content already sourced in the site-preparation pack)*
33. Does excavation include hauling away the extra dirt, or does that cost extra? *(scope-clarity/objection-adjacent)*
34. How long does excavation take once site prep and permits are done? *(answered, reused from homepage pack: often just a few days for residential; commercial/multi-building excavation commonly runs 1-3+ weeks)*
35. How long does a full excavation project take for a new driveway or pond? *(project-specific timeline — no single authoritative figure found; framed as depending on length/depth/soil per the driveway and pond cost sources above)*

## decision / residential vs commercial

36. What's the difference between residential and commercial excavation? *(directly named in topical map §6.2 FAQ list — answered: residential jobs typically use compact machines — mini excavators, skid steers — with one or two operators moving at most a few hundred cubic yards; commercial jobs are measured in acres, use full-size excavators/dozers/graders with multiple simultaneous crews, mass-grade to engineered elevations, and involve stricter regulatory compliance)*
37. Does a residential excavation job take less time than a commercial one? *(answered: yes, typically — a residential job can wrap in a week or less, while commercial excavation stretches over weeks to months depending on scope)*
38. Will a small residential job get the same attention as a big commercial contract? *(objection-adjacent, reused theme from homepage pack — trust concern about being deprioritized)*
39. Do I need an excavation contractor or can I DIY it with a rented excavator? *(decision-triggering DIY-vs-hire threshold — answered directionally: rental is viable for very small, shallow, obstacle-free digging, but foundation, utility, and any excavation near property lines/utilities/slopes carries real liability and skill requirements that favor hiring a licensed contractor)*
40. What size of job is too small for an excavation company to bother with? *(reused verbatim from homepage pack question #3, scoped here to excavation specifically)*

## oregon_specific

41. Do I need to call before I dig in Oregon? What is Oregon811? *(oregon_specific, high-value — answered: yes, required by law; the official one-call center is the Oregon Utility Notification Center (OUNC), operating publicly as "Oregon 811" — digsafelyoregon.com and oregon.gov/puc)*
42. How much advance notice does Oregon811 require before digging? *(oregon_specific — answered: at least 2 business days before starting any digging project, per oregon.gov/puc; holidays do not count toward the notice period)*
43. Is calling Oregon811 free? *(oregon_specific — answered: yes, locate requests are free, per digsafelyoregon.com)*
44. What happens if I dig without calling 811 first in Oregon? *(oregon_specific, liability-fear — answered directionally: the Oregon Public Utility Commission enforces Oregon's excavation/dig laws and can take enforcement action against excavators who skip the one-call system; this research pass found no specific published penalty dollar figure — do not invent one, cite the PUC's enforcement role and recommend confirming exact penalty exposure directly with the PUC if a customer asks)*
45. Do I need a permit to excavate on my own property in Lane County? *(oregon_specific — answered by reusing the site-preparation pack's finding: Lane County's Grading and Fill Permit is triggered by moving more than 50 cubic yards of earth or a cut/fill slope steeper than 2:1, sourced from a third-party guide and flagged for direct Lane County LMD confirmation before publishing as an exact figure; foundation excavation under an active building permit is generally exempt from a separate grading permit — see `07-oregon-authority.md` §3, reused from `../site-preparation/07-oregon-authority.md` §1)*
46. Is there a trench-safety standard excavation contractors have to follow? *(oregon_specific/authority — answered by reusing the homepage pack's OSHA 1926 Subpart P finding verbatim: trenches 5ft deep or more require a protective system; safe access is required within 25ft of lateral travel for excavations 4ft deep or more — see `07-oregon-authority.md` §2, reused from `../home/07-oregon-authority.md` §4)*

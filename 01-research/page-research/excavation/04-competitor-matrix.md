This file re-examines the homepage pack's 8 competitors (Danco, All Dirt, Heavy D, Emerald, McKenzie, Braun, Strasheim, A&D) specifically for what they say about **general excavation/grading/earthmoving**, rather than re-discovering competitors from scratch, per the task brief. Two supplemental competitors surface organically and are added as reference points: **BBC Excavation Inc** (already added in the site-preparation pack, and independently relevant here since it names pond and pool excavation specifically) and **Tectonic Construction** (`tectonicoutdoorliving.com`, formerly `oregontectonic.com` — 301-redirected — which surfaced directly ranking for "excavation contractor eugene oregon" with a dedicated `/excavation/` page).

**Method note:** Same as prior packs — `WebFetch` against live pages, summarized by an intermediate model rather than hand-parsed HTML. Treat heading/structure counts as directionally reliable, not pixel-exact.

## Matrix B — Does the Homepage/Site-Prep Packs' "0/8-9 Structural Depth" Finding Hold for Excavation Specifically?

**Yes, and it sharpens in a new direction.** The homepage and site-preparation packs both found zero competitors run a real FAQ, publish pricing, or provide permit guidance. That holds here too. But this page's research surfaces two *new* angles the sibling packs couldn't have found because they're excavation-specific: **(1) only All Dirt names the full topical-map-validated process list as explicit line items — every other competitor, including some with dedicated excavation pages, describes excavation in vague, single-sentence terms**, and **(2) pond excavation, a named project type in both the topical map and the task brief, is claimed by only 2 of 10 competitors and explained by none of them.** Neither the homepage pack nor the site-preparation pack could have surfaced the pond-specificity gap, since it only shows up when competitors' excavation-specific (not site-prep-specific) content is examined directly.

## Individual Notes Specific to Excavation

**All Dirt, Inc.** — Confirmed again as "the model to beat," and the gap between its page and a genuinely complete excavation page (FAQ with real content, pricing, equipment, pond/driveway/utility project-type depth, Oregon811, residential-vs-commercial explanation) remains large and entirely fillable.

**Tectonic Construction** — The most interesting new find of this pass. A dedicated, project-type-specific excavation page (home pads, driveway/road excavation, utility trenching, tight-access work) with real equipment specificity (mini excavators, conveyor belt) that no other competitor in either the original 8 or the site-prep pack's supplemental BBC addition demonstrates. A genuine second reference point alongside All Dirt for what "good" looks like in this market — still with zero pricing, zero FAQ, and zero Oregon811/permit content.

**McKenzie Excavating & BBC Excavation Inc** — The only two competitors (of 10) to name pond work at all, and neither explains cost, process, or Oregon's OWRD/DSL permitting framework. This is the clearest single project-type opportunity for D&D to own outright.

**Danco, Heavy D, Emerald, Braun, Strasheim, A&D** — Collectively confirm that "excavation and grading" is treated as a single blended service by most of this market, consistent with the pattern already noted in the homepage and site-preparation packs — reinforcing that this page's job is to be the first to draw a clear, useful, non-confusing line between excavation and its sibling Grading & Leveling page.

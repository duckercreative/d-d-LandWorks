## 1. Lane County Is a DEQ Agent County (confirmed — carry forward from base pack)

**Lane County is not one of the three counties where Oregon DEQ directly manages septic permitting.** DEQ manages permitting directly in Curry, Jackson, and Josephine counties only. In all other 33 Oregon counties — including Lane County — DEQ has authorized local jurisdictions to act as its agent.

- Under **OAR 340-071-0120 (Jurisdiction and Policy)**, DEQ may enter into agreements authorizing local jurisdictions to act as DEQ's agent for onsite wastewater permitting.
- **Lane County's On-Site Wastewater program** (within the Land Management Division, Public Works) is the local agent. Their stated mission: regulating "the installation, repair, alteration, and maintenance of onsite wastewater disposal systems (septic systems)" for the county.
- The office address is **3050 N. Delta Hwy, Eugene, OR 97408**; phone **(541) 682-4651**, Mon–Fri 9am–3pm (sanitation staff by appointment only).
- Applications submitted online through Lane County's **ePASS portal**.

**Confidence level:** High. Multiple convergent sources (OAR text, Lane County's own program page, confirmed by live 2026-09-24 research). See caveat in `../septic/07-oregon-authority.md` §1 about Lane County not appearing on a directly-quoted DEQ county agent roster page in the 2026-09-08 pass — re-verify against `oregon.gov/deq/residential/pages/onsite-contacts.aspx` before publishing as an unattributed absolute.

## 2. Lane County Septic Permit Fee — Confirmed [NEW 2026-09-24]

**Confirmed current fee:** The Lane County Septic Site Evaluation fee is **$866**, effective September 1, 2026 under Board Order 26-07-14-17. This is the first step in the installation permit process and must be paid before Lane County's Subsurface Sanitation Program conducts the site evaluation.

**What is included in the $866 site evaluation fee:**
- County staff review the submitted site plan and test pit documentation
- Site evaluation appointment (test pits must be dug by the applicant/contractor in advance)
- Written determination of system type, size, and location approved for the property
- Site Evaluation Permit issued (not the construction permit — that is a separate, subsequent application)

**What is NOT included:**
- The cost of digging the test pits (done by the contractor — typically a few hundred dollars of excavation time)
- The Construction Permit fee (a separate, subsequent fee — see §3 below)
- Any planning review or land use clearance fee
- Electrical permit for ATT pump systems (separate from the septic permit)

**Previously-cited secondary-source range ($866–$998):** The lower end ($866) is now confirmed as the Lane County current fee. The upper end may reflect a contingency or a different fee schedule year. Use **$866** as the current figure, labeled as effective September 2026.

**Sources:**
- Lane County Board Order 26-07-14-17 (September 2026 fee schedule update), confirmed via live 2026-09-24 research
- [Willamette Craftworks — Lane County Building Permits Guide](https://willamettecraftworks.com/permitting/lane-county-or/) (secondary source)

## 3. The Two-Step Lane County Permit Process (install-specific) [UPDATED 2026-09-24]

Lane County's installation permitting follows a two-step sequence that this page must explain clearly — zero competitors currently explain this process to homeowners.

**Step 1 — Site Evaluation Permit:**
1. Submit application via ePASS portal (online) or in person at 3050 N. Delta Hwy
2. Submit a site plan (plot plan showing proposed and existing development, wells, property lines, proposed test pit locations)
3. Dig test pits before the site evaluation appointment:
   - Minimum 2 pits (3 preferred), spaced 50–100 ft apart
   - Each pit: 2 ft wide × 4 ft long × 5 ft deep, with stepped/ramped access for evaluator safety
4. Lane County evaluates soil morphology in the pits: soil texture, structure, color, mottling (water table indicators), and depth to impermeable layers
5. Lane County issues the Site Evaluation Permit with: approved system type (conventional or ATT), approved size, and approved location
6. **Review timeline:** Lane County targets within 20 days for a complete application
7. **Fee:** $866 (effective September 2026)

**Step 2 — Construction (Installation) Permit:**
1. Apply after the Site Evaluation Permit is approved
2. Must also obtain final land use planning clearance before the Construction Permit is issued
3. Lane County issues the Construction Permit with specific installation instructions and a detailed site diagram
4. **Permit validity:** 1 year — installation must be completed within this period
5. A separate electrical permit is required for any ATT system using a pump

**Step 3 — Installation and Inspection:**
1. DEQ-certified installer (or property owner with restrictions) performs the work
2. Lane County inspection required at key stages:
   - After excavation, before backfilling the tank
   - During or immediately after drainfield installation
3. Final inspection required to "close out" the permit
4. Physical installation timeline: **3–5 days** for a typical residential system (confirmed from All Dirt's FAQ, 2026-09-24); more complex or ATT systems may take longer

**How to apply:** [Lane County ePASS portal](https://www.lanecountyor.gov/epass) for online applications; in-person at 3050 N. Delta Hwy, Eugene, OR 97408.

**Sources:**
- [Lane County — Subsurface Sanitation Permits General Information](https://www.lanecounty.org/government/county_departments/public_works/land_management_division/on-_site_wastewater/subsurface_sanitation_permits_general_information)

## 4. Oregon DEQ Credential Categories (reused from base pack — confirmed correct)

**Reused verbatim from `../septic/07-oregon-authority.md` §2 — do not re-derive:**

Oregon's real DEQ credential structure:

**Business licenses (Sewage Disposal Service license) — three types:**
1. **Installer** license — for businesses constructing/installing septic systems
2. **Pumper** license — for businesses pumping and disposing of septic tank contents
3. **Combined Pumper/Installer** license — for businesses doing both

Bond requirement: $15,000 for Installer or Combined licenses; $5,000 for Pumper-only.

**Individual certifications:**
1. **Installer Certification** — required before a business can hold an Installer or Combined license; at least one member/employee must hold this; obtained via DEQ's Initial Installer Certification course (2-day, delivered through Chemeketa Community College; 70% minimum exam pass); recertification every 3 years (18 approved CEUs)
2. **Maintenance Provider Certification** — separate credential required specifically for servicing ATT/sand-filter systems on an ongoing basis; not required for installation only

**Confirmed from live 2026-09-24 research:**
- Chemeketa Community College confirmed as primary DEQ Installer Certification course provider
- Oregon DEQ maintains a **public searchable database** at oregon.gov/deq/residential/pages/onsite-search.aspx (link directly from this page — no competitor does this)

**Content constraint — do NOT assert David's specific license tier:** business-info.md confirms D&D holds "DEQ Certified" status. The specific business license category (Installer vs. Combined Pumper/Installer) and David's individual certification are not confirmed. This page can explain the general credential structure as Oregon regulatory education without asserting which specific tier D&D holds.

## 7. Rural Lane County — Regulatory and Geographic Context [NEW 2026-09-24]

**Lane County's own official language:** "Many homes and businesses in the County are in rural areas without access to municipal sewer systems." This directly validates D&D's rural positioning as a natural, government-acknowledged need rather than a niche claim.

**On-Site Wastewater program scope:** The Lane County program covers **all unincorporated areas** of the county — any property outside the incorporated city limits of Eugene, Springfield, or other incorporated cities.

**Urban Growth Boundary (UGB) as a practical dividing line:** Properties outside a city's UGB generally cannot connect to city sewer even if they are relatively close to a city. The UGB defines the boundary beyond which municipal services are not extended.

**Rural communities confirmed to rely primarily on on-site septic systems (from live 2026-09-24 research):**
- Veneta, Junction City, Creswell, Cottage Grove, Lowell (confirmed by All Dirt and other competitors as septic-reliant communities)
- Elmira, Noti, Crow, Lorane, Dexter, Fall Creek (western and southern Lane County rural areas)
- Deadwood, Marcola (noted as rural septic reliance communities)
- McKenzie River area / McKenzie Bridge corridor (rural eastern Lane County)
- Oakridge (mountain community; likely no city sewer)
- Florence area (coastal Lane County)
- Dorena, Culp Creek (south Lane County rural)

**Key rural-specific regulatory considerations:**
- **Well setback requirements:** Rural Lane County properties with private wells face setback requirements between the well and any part of the septic system. Oregon's general standard requires a minimum horizontal separation distance; Lane County's Subsurface Sanitation Program enforces this during the site evaluation. Content should mention this without specifying an exact setback distance unless confirmed against current OAR text.
- **Drainfield protection rule (reused from `../site-preparation/07-oregon-authority.md`):** Once a drainfield location is proposed or approved, the area must NOT be cut, filled, paved, or have a well drilled nearby — doing so can void the site's septic approval. This is especially important for rural properties where development sequencing is more complex. Cross-link to the site-preparation page.
- **ATT requirement in challenging rural soils:** Forested areas (tree root intrusion), areas near rivers or wetlands (high water table), clay-heavy terrain, and rocky mountain soils can all force an ATT system rather than a conventional installation — and the cost difference is substantial. Rural buyers need to understand this before committing to a property purchase.

## 9. Homeowner Self-Install Option (confirmed — must be stated accurately)

**Lane County's official guidance states:** it is "generally advisable to have the work completed by a licensed installer," but confirms that a homeowner may perform the work themselves on their own property.

**Oregon DEQ's position:** A homeowner can install a septic system on their own property without a licensed installer license, under specific conditions (own primary residence; not a rental or speculative property; meets all DEQ/Lane County technical standards).

**Content guidance:** State this honestly rather than implying it isn't an option. The DEQ business-license search tool provides homeowners a way to verify any installer they consider, whether it's a professional or themselves. Do NOT present this as a common or recommended approach — Lane County's own guidance uses "generally advisable" to lean toward licensed contractors.

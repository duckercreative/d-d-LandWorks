## Table 1 — Buyer Segment Breakdown

This page targets a fundamentally different buyer than the general septic service page. Where the repair page addresses distressed/crisis buyers (failing system, sewage backup, emergency), this page addresses **planned-installation buyers** — people who know they need a new septic system and are now evaluating contractors.

| Buyer Segment | Funnel Stage | Primary Anxiety | What They Need to Convert | Approx. % of This Page's Audience |

## Table 3 — Trust Killers Specific to This Page (Install-Intent Audience)

The repair page's distressed-buyer trust killers (crisis pricing, exploitation during emergency, pressure to replace vs. repair) do not apply here. These are the install-buyer-specific trust killers:

| Trust Killer | Why it's specific to install buyers | Rebuttal to build into the page |
|---|---|---|
| "Will my rural Lane County property even pass a perc test?" | Pre-build land buyers genuinely don't know; risk of buying land that can't support a system | Explain what the test involves and what happens if it fails — ATT systems are often available as a backup |
| "I don't know if I need a conventional or alternative system — will the contractor push me toward the more expensive one?" | System selection feels like a sales pitch when the buyer doesn't understand it | Explain plainly: system type follows the site evaluation results, not a sales preference |
| "How much is this going to cost — why won't anyone give me a number?" | New septic install is a $5,000–$30,000+ decision; zero competitors publish real ranges | Publish real ranges clearly labeled as general — separated by system type |
| "Is this contractor actually DEQ certified — how do I verify it?" | Homeowners have no independent way to check unless they know where to look | Link directly to DEQ's business license search tool |
| "I'm also getting site prep done — do I need separate contractors, or can one company do both?" | Rural/new-construction buyers especially want to minimize contractor coordination | State explicitly: D&D handles both the excavation/earthwork AND the septic install — one company, one phone call |
| "How long will this take? I'm trying to time it with my building permit." | New construction buyers are coordinating multiple trades; timing matters | Publish real timeline expectations: physical install 3-7 days; permit process weeks to months |
| "My property is outside Eugene — does D&D serve rural areas?" | D&D's current GBP/site marketing may skew Eugene-centric; rural buyers may assume they're out of range | State the full Lane County service area explicitly, naming rural communities |
| "Will the county or DEQ tell me my system failed inspection?" | First-time install buyers don't know what post-install inspection looks like | Explain the Lane County inspection process in the permit section |

## Note on Repair Content Exclusion

This page intentionally excludes repair, distressed-buyer, and "signs of failure" content. A searcher landing on this page via "septic installation Lane County Oregon" has signaled a purchase intent for a new system, not a repair consultation. The CTA and footer should offer a soft cross-link to the general septic page for homeowners who arrive wondering about repair vs. full replacement — but the body copy should not duplicate that page's distressed-buyer framing, per this project's cannibalization-prevention methodology.

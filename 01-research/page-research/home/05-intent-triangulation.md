# Intent Triangulation — D&D Land Works Homepage

**Sourcing note:** Named-competitor 1-3 star review excerpts (the HVAC methodology's preferred source) could not be retrieved directly — Google/Yelp review content is not indexable/fetchable through the tools available in this pass, and no specific negative review text for any of the 8 competitors surfaced in general web search (see `02-question-mine.md` objection section — the search returned only positive-review summaries and directory listings). This is reported honestly rather than papered over with invented quotes. In place of scraped reviews, this file triangulates intent from: (1) the general home-services / excavation hiring-guide literature (which reflects aggregated, real complaint patterns across the industry), (2) Oregon-specific structural facts (climate, soil, regulation) that predictably create pain points, and (3) the CCB's own stated consumer-protection function, which implies the complaint categories it exists to address.

| Pain point | Source | Severity | Addressed in current competitor content? |

## Table 3 — Trust Killers & Rebuttals

| Trust killer | Why it matters here | D&D rebuttal (explicit, evidence-based — no invented stats) |
|---|---|---|
| "Licensed and bonded" as an unverifiable slogan | Every one of the 8 competitors uses similar language with no way to check it | State the exact CCB number (#261742) and link directly to the official Oregon CCB lookup tool (search.ccb.state.or.us) so a visitor can verify it themselves in one click |
| Vague "we do everything" positioning that turns out to exclude key services once you call | All Dirt/Danco list broad service sets that blur into unrelated trades (tree removal, hauling) | Publish an honest, specific service list and do not claim tree removal, stump grinding, paving, or septic pumping — scope precision is itself a trust signal |
| No way to know what a job will roughly cost before calling | Zero competitors publish any price signal | Publish real cost RANGES sourced from this research pack's cost data (clearly labeled as general Lane County ranges, not a quote) rather than hiding entirely behind "call for a free estimate" |
| Fear that a small residential job won't get real attention | Implied by "small excavation contractor" and "residential vs commercial" search behavior | State plainly that residential is not an afterthought — explicitly welcome smaller-scope jobs on the homepage |
| No visible septic-specific credential explanation | "DEQ Certified" is stated as a badge everywhere it appears (business card, business-info.md) but never explained | Add a one-paragraph, plain-language explanation of what DEQ certification legally requires and why it qualifies D&D specifically for septic work |
| Rainy-season timeline risk not acknowledged | Zero competitors address Oregon's defining seasonal construction constraint | Proactively explain the May-October preferred window and how D&D handles wet-season jobs (extra rock, tighter erosion control) rather than letting the customer discover this mid-project |
| Zero reviews / zero online presence at launch | New, unavoidable reality for D&D | Do not fabricate testimonials or a review count. Lean on the explicit CCB license link, the DEQ certification explanation, and (once available) a small number of real early-customer photos/quotes as they accumulate honestly |

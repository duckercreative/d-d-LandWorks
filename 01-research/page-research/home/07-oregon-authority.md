## 2. Oregon DEQ — Onsite Wastewater (Septic) Program

- **Governing rules:** Oregon Administrative Rules (OAR) Chapter 340, Division 071 govern the permitting, design, installation, and operation of septic/onsite wastewater systems statewide.
- **Certification structure:** DEQ maintains two relevant professional certifications — **Installer Certification** (required to legally construct, install, repair, or maintain septic systems) and **Maintenance Provider Certification** (ongoing system maintenance contracts, recertified every 3 years). D&D's "DEQ Certified" positioning almost certainly refers to Installer Certification — **confirm the exact certificate name/number with David before publishing a specific claim.**
- **2025 rule update:** DEQ's Environmental Quality Commission adopted updated onsite wastewater rules in 2025 addressing sewer availability and accessory dwelling unit (ADU) issues stemming from 2023 legislation, plus general modernization of program language — relevant context for any ADU-adjacent site-prep content.
- **Source:** [Oregon DEQ — Rules and Regulations for Onsite Wastewater Treatment Systems](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/residential/pages/onsite-rules.aspx) · [Oregon DEQ — Installer and Maintenance Provider Certification](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/residential/pages/onsite-certification.aspx) · [Oregon DEQ — Onsite Wastewater Management Program 2025 Adopted Rules](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/rulemaking/adopted/Pages/Onsite2025.aspx) · verified 2026-09-08

## 3. Lane County Permit Requirements (Grading / Excavation / Driveways)

- **Foundation excavation:** Excavation specifically for a building foundation, when authorized under a valid building permit, is generally exempt from a separate grading permit — but driveway and landscaping grading is **not** automatically exempt.
- **Driveway/access work:** Creating a new driveway, paving an existing gravel apron, or modifying how a driveway connects to a county road triggers permit requirements, including specific sight-distance and culvert-sizing standards so the driveway doesn't flood the public roadside ditch. This directly informs `/driveway-repair/` content and the homepage's permit-guidance FAQ.
- **Right-of-way work:** Any work inside the public right-of-way (utility trenching, temporary construction fencing) is handled by Lane County's Land Management Division (LMD) and Public Works — relevant to `/utility-excavation/` content.
- **General standard:** Lane County code directs that grading, excavation, and clearing be avoided when detrimental to soil stability and erosion control — a citable, official framing for why D&D's erosion-conscious, season-aware approach matters.
- **City of Eugene specific:** A site development permit within Eugene city limits carries a base charge of $963.56 (covering Public Works + Land Use review), with additional fees possible depending on project scope.
- **Recommendation:** Because exemptions and thresholds vary by exact project type, the homepage/FAQ content should state the general rule and explicitly recommend contacting Lane County Building & Development Services (or the relevant city) to confirm permit status for a specific project — this is both accurate and a trust-building move (nobody in the competitor set does this).
- **Source:** [Lane County Code Chapter 16](https://cdnsm5-hosted.civiclive.com/UserFiles/Servers/Server_3585797/File/Government/County%20Departments/County%20Counsel/Lane%20Code/LC16.001_015.pdf) · [Willamette Craftworks — Lane County Building Permits: 2026 Homeowner's Guide](https://willamettecraftworks.com/permitting/lane-county-or/) · [Eugene, OR — Site Development FAQ](https://www.eugene-or.gov/faq.aspx?TID=28) · verified 2026-09-08

## 4. OSHA / Oregon OSHA — Trench & Excavation Safety

- **Governing standard:** 29 CFR 1926, Subpart P ("Excavations") — federal OSHA standard covering all excavation and trenching operations, enforced in Oregon through Oregon OSHA (a state-plan program).
- **Key thresholds:**
  - A "trench" is defined as a narrow excavation, deeper than it is wide, with a bottom width not exceeding 15 feet.
  - Trenches **5 feet deep or more** require a protective system (shoring, shielding, or sloping) unless excavated entirely in stable rock.
  - Safe access (ladder, steps, or ramp) is required within 25 feet of lateral travel for any excavation **4 feet deep or more**.
  - A "competent person" (a specific OSHA term of art — someone capable of identifying existing/predictable hazards and authorized to take corrective action) must be involved in decisions about protective systems for excavations under 5 feet.
- **Why cite this:** Zero competitors in the 8-site matrix mention trench safety standards anywhere. Referencing OSHA Subpart P by name is a genuine, differentiating authority signal that also happens to be true and directly relevant to D&D's utility/trenching and foundation work.
- **Source:** [OSHA — 1926 Subpart P (Excavations)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926SubpartP) · [OSHA — Trenching and Excavation Overview](https://www.osha.gov/trenching-excavation) · verified 2026-09-08

## 5. Willamette Valley Climate & Soil (Eugene-specific)

- **Rainfall pattern:** Per NOAA's 1991-2020 climate normals, Eugene's wettest months are December (7.21 in.), January (6.05 in.), and November (5.98 in.). Annual normal precipitation is **40.83 inches**. Rain begins increasing in mid-to-late September and tapers through late June; July-August are typically very dry.
- **Construction implication — dry-season window:** Multiple independent contractor-industry sources converge on **May through October** as the preferred dry-season window for excavation, grading, and compaction-dependent work in the Willamette Valley; the wet season (roughly November-April) is workable but slower, costlier, and requires additional rock/erosion-control measures.
- **Soil profile:** Willamette Valley soils are widely documented (OSU Extension, regional gardening/construction sources) as clay-heavy: high water-holding capacity, poor pore space/drainage, prone to surface ponding, hardens when dry and becomes sticky/heavy when wet. Digging wet clay creates a subgrade that "pumps and ruts" under equipment and won't compact reliably; workarounds include timber/composite mats, gravel working pads, and scheduling clay-dependent work for the dry window.
- **Why this matters for the homepage:** This is the most Oregon-specific, competitor-unaddressed authority signal available (0/8 competitors mention seasonal scheduling or soil type anywhere). It directly explains real customer pain points (driveway washout, yard flooding, cost variability) with genuine, sourced regional data rather than generic contractor boilerplate.
- **Source:** [NOAA / National Weather Service — Eugene Climate Data](https://www.weather.gov/media/pqr/climate/ClimateBookEugene/pg61.pdf) · [OSU Extension Service — Clay Soil Challenges and Solutions for Oregon Gardeners](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/news/clay-soil-challenges-solutions-oregon-gardeners) · [Cojo Asphalt — Digging in Wet Clay: The Problems and the Workarounds](https://www.cojoasphalt.com/blog/digging-wet-clay-soil-problems-oregon) · verified 2026-09-08

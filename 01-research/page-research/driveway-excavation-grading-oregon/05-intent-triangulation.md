## Seasonal Context — Lane County

**Willamette Valley wet season (October–April):**
- ~47" annual rainfall in Eugene, concentrated Oct–Apr
- Clay subgrade loses strength when saturated — worst time for base work
- Emergency re-grading is possible year-round but may need follow-up pass once ground dries

**Dry season (May–September):**
- Best window for major excavation, base prep, and new driveway installation
- Clay subgrade is firm and compactable
- Timeline advantage: complete before wet season starts

**Seasonal content strategy:**
- In spring (March–May): "Now is the time to repair winter damage before ground softens again"
- In fall (Aug–Oct): "Schedule before the wet season — once clay saturates, scheduling and compaction quality both suffer"
- These are genuine planning facts; no competitor currently uses this angle

## Intent Pattern Summary (7 Key Signals)

1. **Cost queries dominate mid-funnel** — "How much does" variations appear in every cluster. Lead with cost content; don't bury it. Competitors who put pricing early earn featured snippets.
2. **Failure-mode queries are highest-urgency, fastest-converting** — "Washed out," "ruts," "potholes," "washboard," "sinking" queries signal active problems. These homeowners call faster than planning queries.
3. **Oregon clay soil is the differentiating local angle** — Multiple search clusters and competitors cite Willamette Valley clay. D&D should own this explicitly: geotextile fabric, deeper excavation, why it matters.
4. **"Near me" and city-specific queries are highest-converting local terms** — Name Eugene, Springfield, Cottage Grove, Creswell, Junction City in copy and optionally in an H2 service-area section.
5. **Competitor gap: repair content underdeveloped for Eugene** — Most competitors have thin service lists. Repair + failure-mode + drainage content has no strong local owner.
6. **Permit questions are trust-building opportunity** — PAA surfaces permit queries consistently; no local contractor addresses them. A 100-word permit section captures a featured snippet cluster and signals expertise.
7. **Material research = close-to-hire** — Queries for "3/4 minus gravel," "geotextile fabric," "base depth" come from users in active planning stages. Include material context in body copy, not just FAQ.

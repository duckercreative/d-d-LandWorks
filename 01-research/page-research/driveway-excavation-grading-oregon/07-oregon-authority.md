## §1 — Gravel Driveway Build Specs (Lane County / Willamette Valley)

No published ODOT or Lane County residential gravel driveway depth standard exists — ODOT's Standard Specifications (2024) govern paved public road construction only. Verified depth specs below are industry consensus, confirmed across multiple Oregon contractor sources and converge consistently.

| Layer | Material | Depth | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subgrade prep | Non-woven geotextile fabric over native clay | — | Mandatory on Willamette Valley clay; without it, base rock pumps into clay within 1–2 wet seasons |
| Base course | 2–4 inch minus crushed rock (angular, compacted in 4–6" lifts) | 6–12 inches | 6" minimum for passenger use on stable subgrade; 8–12" standard on Lane County clay |
| Wearing course | 3/4 minus crushed quarry rock | 2–4 inches | Oregon standard surface material; "the State of Oregon's standard top-layer for all gravel roads" (Lane Forest Products) |

**Heavy-use note:** Equipment pads, farm driveways, or delivery vehicle access warrant 10–12 inches base or more.

**Frame as:** industry practice (not government-mandated spec). ODOT's pavement design standards govern public roads; residential gravel driveway depth is contractor-industry consensus with no residential code equivalent.

**Oregon vocabulary to use:**
- "3/4 minus" — standard surface material name in Oregon; use this term, not "pea gravel" or "aggregate"
- "2–4 inch minus" or "base rock" — base course term
- "Road base" / "CSBC" (Crushed Surface Base Course) — interchangeable with base rock; both appear in Oregon contractor content
- "Crushed quarry rock" — preferred in Eugene/Willamette Valley supply chain (Lane Forest Products uses this term)
- "Geotextile fabric" or "road fabric" — Oregon contractor standard term for woven separator layer
- "Pit run" — common east of the Cascades; less common in Lane County; avoid as primary term for this page

**Compaction:** Material placed and compacted in 4–6 inch lifts. Critical in wet-climate applications — uncompacted material shifts when saturated.

## §2 — Permit Requirements — Three Layers

### A. Lane County ROW Access Connections Permit — CONFIRMED REQUIRED

**What triggers it:** Any new or modified approach connecting private property to a Lane County road — new driveway apron, modification of existing apron, removal, repair, or evaluation of an approach.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Permit type | Facility/Access Connections Permit |
| Fee | $250 temporary; **$450 permanent** |
| Governing code | Lane Code Chapter 15 |
| Application | Online through Lane County Public Works; staff contacts applicant for fees before issuance |
| Standards covered | Width, location, number of approaches, surface type, culvert sizing |
| Surface note | Gravel listed as acceptable; asphalt/concrete may be required on paved roads |
| Contact | [email protected] · (541) 682-6902 · 3050 N. Delta Hwy, Eugene, OR 97408 |

**Source:** Lane County ROW Permits page (lanecountyor.gov/government/county_departments/public_works/right-of-way_permits); 2024 Facility Permit Application PDF confirmed.

**Content implication:** Work grading an existing driveway on private property without modifying the road approach generally does NOT require a permit. The permit is triggered by approach modification, not regrading.

### B. ODOT Highway Approach Permit — REQUIRED for state highway frontage

**What triggers it:** Any new private approach to an Oregon state highway (ORS 374.305(1)) — applies when a property fronts Highway 99, Highway 58, Highway 126, or any ODOT-designated route in Lane County.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Application form | Form 734-2680, "Application for State Highway Approach" |
| Submit to | ODOT District 5 office (Lane County) |
| Timeline | ODOT notifies within 30 days if additional info needed |
| Governing statute | ORS 374.305(1) |

**Source:** ODOT Access Management page (oregon.gov/odot/engineering/pages/access-management.aspx); ODOT Permit Process PDF (oregon.gov/odot/Engineering/AccessMgmt/PermitProcess.pdf).

### C. City of Eugene — Stormwater and Erosion Review

**Within Eugene city limits:** City of Eugene issues residential construction permits that may cover driveway work. The Stormwater Management Manual and Erosion Prevention Program apply to new driveway construction in city limits.

**Unincorporated Lane County** (most rural D&D jobs): Lane County road approach permit governs. City of Eugene rules apply within Eugene city limits only.

**Contact:** Residential Plan Review (541) 682-5611 | residentialpermitinfo@eugene-or.gov

**Source:** eugene-or.gov/858/Residential; excavationoregon.com/permit-to-build-a-driveway/.

### Bottom-Line Content Sentence

> "Connecting a new driveway to a Lane County road requires a Lane County ROW Access Connections Permit ($450). If your property fronts a state highway, ODOT requires a separate highway approach permit under ORS 374.305(1). Work grading an existing driveway entirely on private property typically needs no permit."

## §3 — Lane County Soil Profile

**Dominant soil type:** Malpass silty clay loam (and associated series: Amity, Woodburn, Dayton) across the Willamette Valley floor — Eugene metro, Junction City, Creswell.

**Hydrologic Soil Group:** D — "very slow rate of water transmission," high shrink-swell potential, seasonally high water table.

**Practical driveway effects:**
- Wet season: clay holds water and loses load-bearing strength when saturated → base rock sinks into clay within 1–2 wet seasons without geotextile separator
- Dry season: clay shrinks and contracts → differential movement creates surface irregularity
- Geotextile fabric on Malpass clay: not optional — a functional requirement. Driveways built without it on clay subgrade fail within two winters.

**Cojo Asphalt quote (Albany, Linn County — same Valley floor geology):** "Clay behaves in two bad ways for driveways: in the wet season it holds water and loses strength. In summer it dries and shrinks."

**Source:** USDA Web Soil Survey (Lane County); OSU Extension soil survey guide; cojoasphalt.com/blog/digging-wet-clay-soil-problems-oregon.

### What Drives Costs Up in Oregon

1. Willamette Valley clay requiring deeper excavation + geotextile fabric
2. Wet-season mobilization difficulties
3. Culvert requirements (nearly universal on driveways longer than 200–300 ft)
4. Fish-bearing stream crossings (ODFW review, engineered culvert required)
5. Long mobilization distances for rural Lane County properties
6. Lane County ROW permit fee for new approaches ($450)

## §7 — Common Lane County Driveway Failure Modes

1. **Gravel migration/washout** — insufficient crown, no ditching, rain channels down driving surface and carries surface rock to low spots; most common failure for rural Valley driveways
2. **Rutting** — base too thin or clay punching through (no geotextile); tire tracks fill with water; primary failure mode on clay subgrades
3. **Edge erosion** — no edging or shoulder grading; gravel falls off sides; common on hillside cuts
4. **Soft spots / mud pumping** — saturated clay pumping up through base rock ("pumping fines"); classic sign of missing geotextile or inadequate base depth
5. **Settlement after freeze-thaw** — ice lens formation then collapse; more common in foothills and Cottage Grove area than Valley floor

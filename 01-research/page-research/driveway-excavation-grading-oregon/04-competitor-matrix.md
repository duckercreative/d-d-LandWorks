## What All 8 Competitors Miss (D&D's Opportunity Set)

1. **Technical depth specs with source framing** — Zero local competitors publish base depth (6–12 inches), surface course (2–4 inches), or material specs. First to publish owns every featured snippet and PAA for spec queries.
2. **Lane County pricing ranges** — Only cojo publishes Oregon-specific cost data (Junction City/Lane County context). Local excavation contractors either say "call for a quote" or give no pricing. Publishing ranges with explanation of what drives variation converts the cost-research segment.
3. **Geotextile fabric + clay soil context** — Only cojo and greenbar mention this; neither is Lane County specific. Every Eugene/Lane County homeowner with a clay driveway has this pain point.
4. **Permit specifics (Lane County $450 + ODOT approach)** — Zero local competitors address this. This question appears in PAA for multiple queries and has no local answer.
5. **Seasonal scheduling content** — Zero competitors tell homeowners the best window (May–October) or why (clay compaction failure in wet season). Simple one-paragraph insert that competitors have completely overlooked.
6. **Direct-question H2 format** — Zero competitors use question-format H2s. This format captures PAA box placements and AI citation positions.
7. **FAQPage schema** — Not deployed by any competitor. First deployment = exclusive rich-result ownership.
8. **Failure-mode content** (why driveways fail, named and explained) — Only cojo blog covers this. Local service pages ignore it. This is the highest-urgency content category.
9. **DIY vs. hire guidance** — No competitor addresses when to DIY and when not to. This earns trust and converts the fence-sitters.
10. **Maintenance cycle / regrading frequency** — No competitor covers how often Lane County driveways need service or why. Answers this question and positions D&D for repeat business.

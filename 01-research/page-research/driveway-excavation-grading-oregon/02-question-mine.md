## FAQ Shortlist — 8 Best Questions with Draft Answers

### Q1: How much does gravel driveway grading cost in Eugene or Lane County, Oregon?
Regrading an existing gravel driveway in Lane County typically runs $800–$1,500 for a standard residential driveway. New driveway installation with full excavation, base prep, and gravel typically ranges from $1.50–$5 per square foot depending on depth required, drainage improvements needed, and haul-off. Eugene's clay soils often require deeper excavation and geotextile fabric, both of which increase material and labor costs. The only reliable way to get an accurate number is a site visit — we offer free estimates throughout Lane County.

### Q2: Why does my gravel driveway keep washing out every winter?
Washouts happen because water is running on your driveway instead of off it. The most common culprits: the surface is flat or crowned the wrong way, there's no roadside ditch so water runs on the drive instead of alongside it, or a culvert is undersized or plugged and backs water up over the driveway. The fix isn't just adding gravel — it's a complete drainage system: crowning the surface correctly, ditching the sides, and culverting low crossings. We assess the drainage pattern during every estimate so the repair lasts through Oregon winters, not just until the next big rain.

### Q3: How deep should a gravel driveway base be in Oregon?
For most of the Willamette Valley, a properly built gravel driveway needs 8–12 inches of compacted base — typically 6–8 inches of 2-inch minus crushed rock, topped with 2–4 inches of 3/4-inch minus surface course. Eugene's clay soil holds moisture and loses strength when saturated, so most contractors install non-woven geotextile fabric between the clay subgrade and the base rock to keep the two from mixing over the years. Skipping this step is one of the most common reasons driveways sink and soften after a few wet winters.

### Q4: What kind of gravel is best for driveways in the Eugene area?
For the Willamette Valley, 3/4-inch minus crushed quarry rock is the standard. It contains angular stone and stone dust that lock together under compaction, forming a stable, water-shedding surface. Avoid pea gravel or rounded river rock for driveways — they don't compact and wash out easily. For the base course, 2-to-4-inch minus crusher run gives the compacted structural layer underneath. We source all material from regional quarries familiar with Lane County soil conditions.

### Q5: Do I need a permit to install a gravel driveway in Oregon?
It depends on where your driveway connects. Any new approach onto an Oregon state highway requires an ODOT approach permit (Form 734-2680, ORS 374.305(1)). Connecting to a Lane County road requires a Lane County ROW Access Connections Permit ($450 for permanent installations). Oregon law also requires notifying Oregon 811 (Dig Safely Oregon) at least two full business days before any excavation. Work grading an existing driveway entirely on private property, not touching the road approach, generally needs no permit. We advise on permit requirements for your specific address before work begins.

### Q6: How often does a gravel driveway need to be regraded in Oregon?
In Lane County's wet winters, most gravel driveways benefit from regrading every 1–3 years, with an inspection each spring after winter rains subside. High-traffic driveways or driveways with drainage problems can need service every year. Spring (March–May) is the best time to address winter damage; fall (September–October) is ideal to prepare the surface for heavy rain season. Annual maintenance is almost always cheaper than letting a driveway deteriorate to the point of full reconstruction.

### Q7: What is the difference between driveway grading and driveway excavation?
Excavation is the initial cut — removing native soil to create the foundation bed, typically 8–12 inches deep for a new gravel driveway. Grading is the shaping step — establishing the proper slope, crown, and drainage angles so water runs off instead of pooling or eroding the surface. New driveways need both. Regrading an existing driveway that still has a sound base means grading only: redistributing displaced gravel, re-establishing crown, and correcting drainage without excavating the full depth. We assess which scope your driveway needs before quoting — you shouldn't pay for excavation if grading is all that's needed.

### Q8: Can I do gravel driveway repair myself, or do I need to hire a contractor?
Small ruts under an inch deep in a confined spot are a reasonable DIY repair — rake existing material back over the low spot, add a bag or two of matching stone, and compact it by hand or with a plate compactor. Beyond that, diminishing returns set in fast. Fixing washboard, re-establishing crown across a full driveway length, culvert work, and base repair all require a motor grader or box blade. Oregon's clay subgrade and heavy rainfall also mean drainage mistakes compound quickly. If the problem has come back more than twice or covers more than a short stretch, a professional regrade almost always costs less over the next three years.

**What nobody does:** Mention Oregon 811 on their driveway service pages.
**What D&D can do:** Explicitly state: D&D calls Oregon 811 on every project, 2 business days before any digging begins, as required under ORS Chapter 757. Include the 30-day ticket validity change (OAR 952-001-0010(23)) as a current differentiating detail.
**Why it wins:** 0/8 competitors mention this. Any homeowner who has worried about a contractor hitting an unmarked line is converted by this one paragraph.
**Impact:** MEDIUM-HIGH — trust signal + regulatory authority.

### GAP 4 — Lane County Clay / Geotextile Fabric Connection Not Made Locally
**What nobody does:** Connect Malpass clay specifically to the geotextile fabric requirement and explain why.
**What D&D can do:** "Lane County's valley floor sits on Malpass clay — a Hydrologic Group D soil that loses load-bearing strength when saturated. Without geotextile fabric between the clay and the base rock, you're not installing a driveway, you're installing a temporary gravel surface."
**Why it wins:** Cojo Asphalt covers this for Central Oregon; no Eugene-area contractor does. Lane County homeowners searching for why their driveway keeps failing are not finding a local explanation.
**Impact:** HIGH — addresses the #1 pain point (repeated driveway failure) with the actual mechanism, not just the symptom.

### GAP 5 — Failure Mode Diagnosis (Named and Explained)
**What nobody does:** Name and explain each failure mode (washout, rutting, washboard, mud pumping, edge erosion) and what they indicate about the underlying problem.
**What D&D can do:** A "Why Oregon Driveways Fail" or "What Went Wrong" section that names each failure type and its root cause — not just "your driveway needs repair."
**Why it wins:** Every failure-mode query ("gravel driveway keeps washing out," "ruts in gravel driveway," "washboard") is mid-to-high urgency with fast conversion. A page that diagnoses before selling earns the trust that converts these visitors.
**Impact:** HIGH — the second-highest-urgency content cluster after cost.

**What nobody does:** Tell homeowners when to schedule driveway work and why the timing matters in Oregon.
**What D&D can do:** "Best window for major driveway work in Lane County: May–September. Once clay saturates (October–April), base compaction quality drops and scheduling becomes unpredictable. Spring re-grading (March–May) handles winter damage while the ground is still firm."

### GAP 8 — Grading vs. Excavation Distinction Explained
**What nobody does:** Clearly distinguish what regrading covers vs. what full excavation covers, so homeowners don't over-buy.
**What D&D can do:** "Regrading = redistributing existing gravel, re-establishing crown, correcting drainage — no digging. Full excavation = removing soil to establish a new base layer. You shouldn't pay for excavation if regrading is all you need — and you shouldn't pay for regrading alone if the base is gone."
**Why it wins:** Danco has this partially. Making this distinction clearly converts the "I don't know what I need" searcher who is otherwise lost.
**Impact:** MEDIUM — reduces buyer confusion; increases estimate request conversion.

### GAP 9 — Honest DIY vs. Hire Guidance
**What nobody does:** Tell homeowners exactly when DIY makes sense and when it doesn't.
**What D&D can do:** DIY threshold section: small isolated ruts (DIY OK) → crown re-establishment, washboard, culvert, base repair (hire). Explain WHY each threshold: equipment need, risk of compounding the problem, clay dynamics.
**Why it wins:** DIY queries ("can I regrade my driveway myself") appear in question mine (Q78, Q79). Honest guidance that includes DIY-OK scenarios builds more trust than always pushing the service.
**Impact:** MEDIUM — trust builder; converts fence-sitters.

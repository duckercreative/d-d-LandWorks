## Oregon Regulatory Authority Signals

### 1. Oregon Construction Contractors Board (CCB)
- **Requirement:** Any contractor paid to excavate, grade, or construct a driveway in Oregon as part of a construction project must hold a current CCB license.
- **Verification:** ccb.oregon.gov — public lookup by license number
- **D&D credential:** CCB #261742 (Licensed and Bonded)
- **On-page use:** Display CCB # in trust bar and footer; FAQ answer on "does a CCB license matter for driveway excavation?"; link to ccb.oregon.gov for verification
- **Citation value:** HIGH — AIO frequently cites CCB requirement for Oregon contractor queries

### 2. Oregon 811 (Call Before You Dig)
- **Requirement:** Oregon law requires notification to Oregon 811 at least 3 business days before any excavation. Utilities must be marked before any digging begins.
- **Enforcement:** Oregon Revised Statutes Chapter 757; Oregon PUC oversight
- **D&D process:** Routine — locate call placed on every job before excavation begins
- **On-page use:** FAQ answer on "What is Oregon 811 and do I need it for a driveway?" or brief mention in process section; link to oregon811.com
- **Citation value:** HIGH — mentioned in AI Overviews for "driveway excavation Oregon"; ZERO competitors mention it on their service pages

### 3. Lane County Facility Permit (Right-of-Way)
- **Requirement:** A Lane County Facility Permit is required for any approach, culvert, or access connecting to a Lane County road. This includes new driveways or changes to existing approaches at county road intersections.
- **Authority:** Lane County Public Works — lanecountyor.gov/government/county_departments/public_works/right-of-way_permits
- **Fee:** Varies; typically $100–$500+ for residential approach
- **On-page use:** FAQ answer on "Do I need a permit to build a driveway in Lane County?"; link to Lane County Public Works for credibility
- **Citation value:** HIGH — zero competitors address this with specificity; AIO gap for Lane County permit question

### 4. City of Eugene Driveway / Approach Permit
- **Requirement:** Projects within Eugene city limits connecting to a city street require an approach permit from the City of Eugene Public Works.
- **Authority:** City of Eugene, Public Works Department
- **On-page use:** Brief mention alongside Lane County permit; distinguish city vs. county requirement

### 5. Oregon DEQ 1200-C Stormwater Permit
- **Requirement:** Any land-disturbing activity affecting 1 or more acres in Oregon requires a 1200-C NPDES stormwater permit from Oregon DEQ. Most residential driveways are under 1 acre and exempt. Larger rural projects (long driveways, site prep + driveway combo) may trigger this.
- **D&D relevance:** Standard site-prep + driveway combo jobs at 1+ acre; D&D is DEQ certified (for septic) and understands DEQ requirements
- **On-page use:** Brief FAQ mention for larger projects; positions D&D as knowledgeable on regulatory compliance
- **Citation value:** MED — relevant for rural property jobs, builds authority signal

## Material Specifications (Oregon-Specific)

### 3/4-Minus Crushed Rock (Road Base)
- **What it is:** Angular crushed rock with "minus" (fines) particles retained. The fines bond under compaction, locking the angular pieces together.
- **Oregon standard:** Used by ODOT and Lane County for gravel road surfaces; the default specification for compactable gravel driveways
- **Common local names:** "Road base," "3/4 minus," "crush and run" (SE US term — not used in Oregon)
- **Where available:** Eugene/Springfield-area gravel suppliers including Lane Forest Products, Quandt Gravel, and others
- **On-page use:** FAQ on "What type of gravel is best?" — answer specifically names 3/4-minus crushed rock

### Base Course vs. Surface Course
- **Base course:** 6–8 inches compacted road base (or bank-run gravel on stable subgrades) — load-bearing layer
- **Surface course:** 3–4 inches 3/4-minus crushed rock — wearing surface, drainable
- **Total depth (Willamette Valley clay):** 10–12 inches total from subgrade to finished surface
- **On-page use:** Process section; depth FAQ answer

### Geotextile Separation Fabric
- **Purpose:** Prevents Willamette Valley clay from pumping up into the gravel base over time. Without it, clay migration causes sinking, muddy sections, and accelerated base failure.
- **Oregon specific relevance:** Willamette Valley clay soils have very high clay content; this is not a universal requirement elsewhere but is near-essential in this geography.
- **On-page use:** Material section; FAQ "Do I need geotextile fabric under my gravel driveway in Oregon?"

## Seasonal Authority Signal

### Oregon Dry Season (May–October)
- **Best time for driveway work:** May through October — soil can dry sufficiently for proper compaction
- **Why it matters:** Compaction of 3/4-minus requires moisture content in a specific range — too wet (typical October–April in Willamette Valley) = can't compact properly; material lifts rather than locks
- **Wet season repairs:** Possible for emergency cases but compaction quality is lower; base course repair often needs follow-up in spring after soils dry
- **Authority citation:** Cojoasphalt Oregon blog explicitly states "May–October dry window" — use same framing with Lane County specificity

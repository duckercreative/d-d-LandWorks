## 1. City of Eugene — Tree Preservation and Removal Standards (Eugene Code 9.6885)

**Verified directly 2026-09-08** against eugene.municipal.codes/EC/9.6885 and the City of Eugene's own "Tree Removal Permits on Private Property" document.

- A permit is required to remove a **"significant tree"** — defined as a live tree with a trunk diameter of at least **8 inches**, measured **diameter at breast height (DBH, 4.5 feet above ground)**.
- Beyond ordinary significant trees, Eugene has an additional protection tier for **"exceptional" or landmark trees**, designated for size, age, or historical value — removing or altering one requires a special permit and may involve a public hearing.
- Application goes through the City of Eugene Planning and Development Department, with a fee, and may include a site visit.
- **Scope limit — critical:** this ordinance applies **within Eugene city limits only.** It does not apply to unincorporated Lane County rural acreage, which is the majority of D&D's likely land-clearing project type.

**Where to cite:** a "Do I need a permit to clear trees in Lane County?" FAQ, framed explicitly around the in-city vs. rural distinction.

## 2. City of Springfield — Tree Felling Permit

**Verified directly 2026-09-08** against springfield-or.gov's Tree Felling Permit page and PDF memo.

- A permit is generally required for tree removal over roughly **6 inches DBH**, for trees in the **public right-of-way**, or for trees designated **heritage or significant**.
- Oregon's general heritage-tree standard commonly sits at **24+ inches DBH** for special designation.
- Administered by the Springfield Development & Public Works Department / Planning Division.
- The city's own page notes a **waived fee** on tree felling permits as of this pass — **verify current fee status before publishing an exact figure**, since fee waivers are the kind of thing that can lapse or change.
- **Scope limit — same as Eugene:** applies within Springfield city limits only.

**Where to cite:** same FAQ as above, contrasted against Eugene's threshold.

## 4. Burning — three-agency structure (the genuinely confusing part)

**Verified directly 2026-09-08** against oregon.gov/odf, lrapa-or.gov, and oregon.gov/deq.

| Agency | Governs | Key rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Oregon Department of Forestry (ODF)** | Slash/debris burning from clearing operations on **forestland** within an ODF protection district | Burn permit required during fire season (some districts year-round); registration generally required at least 7 days before ignition, unless waived; ODF checks weather/wind and the volume/acreage proposed before approving |
| **Lane Regional Air Protection Agency (LRAPA)** | Certain outdoor burning within its own jurisdiction/fire districts inside Lane County | Does **NOT** directly regulate slash burns (that's ODF's role) or agricultural burns (that's Oregon Department of Agriculture's role) — but forest slash open burning is **expressly prohibited** within the Eugene-Springfield Urban Growth Boundary (ESUGB), and certain other fire districts require a letter permit under LRAPA Title 47 §47-020 |
| **Oregon DEQ** | **Domestic/"backyard" burning** of yard debris from a residence with 4 or fewer dwelling units | Must be burned on the property of origin; must be constantly attended with fire-extinguishing equipment on hand; burning stumps to clear land is explicitly listed by DEQ as a regulated form of open burning |

**No specific penalty dollar figure was found in this pass for unpermitted burning under any of the three agencies — do not invent one.** State the enforcement structure, not a fabricated fine amount.

**Where to cite:** a dedicated "Can I burn the debris after clearing?" section, explicitly naming which of the three agencies applies depending on whether the land is forestland (ODF), inside the Eugene-Springfield UGB or another LRAPA-covered fire district (LRAPA), or an ordinary residential yard-debris situation (DEQ).

## 5. Oregon Department of State Lands (DSL) — Removal-Fill Law

**Reused/extended from the excavation pack's pond-permitting research (`../excavation/07-oregon-authority.md` §4), not re-derived from scratch**, per this task's explicit instruction.

- A DSL Removal-Fill permit is required for moving/removing **50+ cubic yards** of material in "waters of the state," including wetlands.
- **Any amount** of material within designated **Essential Salmonid Habitat**, **Oregon Scenic Waterways**, or designated mitigation sites also requires a permit, regardless of the 50-cubic-yard threshold.
- Relevant to land clearing when a wooded lot borders or includes a wetland, stream, or riparian buffer — grubbing stumps or grading near such a feature can trigger this permit even if the clearing itself seems minor.

**Where to cite:** a brief "clearing near water" caution note, cross-linked rather than fully re-explained (the excavation pack already carries the fuller pond-specific version of this finding).

## 6. Oregon Forest Practices Act (FPA) — verified as generally not applicable here

**Verified 2026-09-08** against oregon.gov/odf's own Forest Practices Act page.

- The FPA sets standards for **commercial** tree-harvesting/forest-management activity on Oregon's non-federal forestland (clearcut size limits, replanting requirements for large clearcuts, stream-buffer rules for logging operations).
- It is **generally not triggered** by a one-time residential land-clearing job — clearing a house lot, driveway, or pasture is not the kind of ongoing commercial forestry/timber operation the Act targets.
- **This pack could not find an explicit, plain-language small-acreage residential exemption stated directly by ODF.** Per the task's own instruction not to assume, this is presented as "likely mostly inapplicable to D&D's typical residential/small-acreage jobs, verify directly with ODF" — not as an absolute claim.

**Where to cite:** a short reassurance FAQ ("Does the Forest Practices Act apply to me?") that states the general non-applicability honestly, with the verification caveat intact.

## 7. Oregon Senate Bill 762 — Defensible Space (cross-reference only)

**Verified 2026-09-08** against oregon.gov/osfm.

- SB 762 (2021) directs the Oregon State Fire Marshal to establish minimum defensible-space vegetation-clearing requirements for structures in wildland-urban interface (WUI) zones rated high or extreme wildfire risk, based on the International WUI Code framework.
- This is a property-level, WUI-risk-rating-triggered requirement — **not** a universal Lane County rule, and it applies more centrally to the `/brush-clearing/` sibling page's existing wildfire-risk framing than to full land clearing.

**Where to cite:** at most a one-sentence cross-reference on this page pointing to the brush-clearing page's fuller wildfire-risk content — do not build this out in depth here.

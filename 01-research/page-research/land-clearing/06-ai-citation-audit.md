Consistent with all four prior packs, no direct AI chat-interface querying (ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini/Claude.ai/Copilot) was possible this session — this audit is reasoned from SERP composition and competitor content-gap analysis, a documented and defensible proxy, not a literal 6-system query-and-capture exercise.

---

## 1. SERP composition for core queries

For "land clearing eugene oregon" / "land clearing lane county" / "land clearing cost per acre oregon," the visible result set mixes: (a) local excavation-contractor competitors with dedicated land-clearing pages (Danco, All Dirt, Rotex), (b) national cost-guide/directory content (Angi, HomeGuide, LawnStarter, OWNR OPS, Cojo Asphalt's Oregon-specific guide), and (c) adjacent-category players — arborists (Highland Tree Service) and forestry/timber specialists (Williams Land Improvement) competing from the opposite direction. This is a **moderately listicle/guide-dominated SERP** for the cost-intent queries specifically, though less so for the pure local-commercial-intent queries ("land clearing eugene or"), which stay dominated by local contractor pages.

**Flag A — Listicle-dominated SERP: PARTIALLY CONFIRMED for cost-intent queries only.** The commercial local-intent head term itself is not directory-dominated the way the homepage's broader "excavation contractor" term was, but the cost/informational layer ("how much does land clearing cost") is dominated by national cost-guide content (Angi, HomeGuide, LawnStarter) that D&D cannot outrank directly but can partially capture citation share from by publishing its own honestly-sourced cost section.

## 2. Citation domain patterns

The national cost-guide sites (Angi, HomeGuide, OWNR OPS) are the domains most likely already cited by AI systems answering "how much does land clearing cost" — they are exactly the kind of structured, tabular, frequently-updated cost content that Perplexity/AI Overviews favor. **None of these domains carry any Oregon-specific, let alone Lane-County-specific, cost data** — the one exception found in this pass, Cojo Asphalt's Oregon-specific land-clearing cost guide, is a general contractor content-marketing page, not an authoritative local source. This is a genuine citation-gap opportunity: a page that publishes real, sourced, Oregon-climate-aware cost ranges (citing the "wet climate produces dense understory growth" and "steep slopes add 20-100%+ to cost" factors already identified in this pack's research) has a real shot at being cited over the generic national guides for Lane-County-specific queries.

## 3. Citation gap list

Statements that, if made well and sourced, would be genuinely citable and are not currently made by any competitor or cost-guide site found in this pass:

1. The precise Lane-County-relevant scope distinction between land clearing and brush clearing (most competitors don't explain it at all; Danco is the lone exception with a directly comparable competitor FAQ)
2. Which specific Oregon/Lane-County agency governs which land-clearing-adjacent question (tree ordinance vs. burn permit vs. wetland rule) — this multi-agency structure is genuinely confusing and unaddressed anywhere in the competitive set
3. The Eugene "significant tree" 8-inch-DBH threshold and Springfield's ~6-inch/heritage-24-inch threshold, both cited by name and code section
4. The real cost-tier difference between construction-grade clearing and pasture/acreage-reclamation clearing, with sourced ranges for each
5. A plain-language explanation of the three debris-disposal paths (haul/mulch/burn) and their approximate cost implications

## 4. Featured-snippet-ready answer block draft (140-165 words)

> Land clearing removes trees, stumps, brush, and debris from a property to open it up for construction, pasture, or general use. It's a heavier-scope, more expensive service than brush clearing, which targets only smaller vegetation — blackberry, scotch broom, saplings — without felling standing timber or grubbing stumps. D&D Land Works provides land clearing for residential and commercial properties throughout Eugene, Springfield, and Lane County, Oregon, including wooded lots being prepared for a new build, and overgrown or neglected acreage being reclaimed for pasture or general use. Work typically includes felling and removing trees within the clearing footprint, grubbing stumps and root balls, cutting and hauling brush, chipping or burning debris where permitted, and rough grading the cleared area. Trees and features a customer wants kept can be flagged and preserved during the walk-through. D&D Land Works is licensed and bonded under Oregon Construction Contractors Board license CCB #261742, serving Eugene, Springfield, and the rest of Lane County. Free estimates are available.

Place in the first 30% of the page's body content, immediately after the hero.

## sequencing (the new-construction-anxiety cluster — highest-value for this page per the task brief)

7. Should site prep happen before or after I hire a builder/general contractor? *(answered: site work — the "dirt work" — typically comes after the builder is hired and the permit/design process is underway, but ahead of the actual construction sequence; pre-construction activities like surveys, permits, and site prep are meant to be locked in before vertical construction starts)*
8. Do I need a land survey before site prep can start? *(answered: yes, typically — a topographic survey establishing existing grades, slopes, drainage patterns, and property lines is standard before a grading/site-prep plan is finalized, per multiple grading-industry sources; verify exact requirement with Lane County for a specific parcel)*
9. Will site prep delay my building permit, or does it happen in parallel with permitting? *(new-construction anxiety, direct trust-killer per `05-intent-triangulation.md`)*
10. What happens if the site-prep crew finds a problem (rock, bad soil, buried debris) that wasn't visible before digging started? *(objection/anxiety)*
11. Does site prep need to happen before or after my septic system is evaluated/permitted? *(oregon_specific — answered: the septic test-pit evaluation must happen before the proposed drainfield area is graded, filled, or otherwise disturbed, since disturbing it can void the county's approval — see `07-oregon-authority.md` §5)*
12. Who's responsible for pulling permits — me, my builder, or the site-prep contractor?
13. What order do things happen in: survey, then permits, then clearing, then grading, then compaction?

## cost

14. How much does site preparation cost for a new home in Eugene or Lane County? *(answered: national/statewide ranges converge on roughly $8,000-$50,000+ depending on terrain and site complexity; per-square-foot ranges run $0.11-$0.25/sq ft for an easy flat cleared lot up to $0.60-$1.50+/sq ft for a difficult heavily wooded, rocky, or sloped lot — no Lane-County-specific figure found, flag as general range)*
15. What's the minimum I should budget for site prep before construction costs even start? *(answered: at least $5,000 as a baseline recommendation, per cost-guide sources — scales up sharply with site difficulty)*
16. How much does cut-and-fill grading cost per cubic yard? *(answered: roughly $2.25-$3.00/cubic yard for straightforward cut/fill — cost-guide data)*
17. What does it cost if the crew hits rock during site prep? *(answered: standard excavation runs $5-$15/cubic yard; rock excavation runs $50-$200/cubic yard — a 10-40x jump; blasting or specialized equipment can add $10,000-$50,000+ to a job)*
18. How much extra does clay soil add to site-prep costs? *(oregon_specific — answered: clay soil is documented to add roughly 25-50% to excavation costs industry-wide, directly relevant to Willamette Valley's clay-heavy soil profile)*
19. Is a geotechnical/soil study worth paying for before site prep starts? *(answered: a $2,000-$5,000 geotechnical study is documented to typically prevent 20-50% of cost overruns on a difficult site — reframes the upsell as budget protection)*
20. Why do site-prep quotes vary so much between contractors for what looks like the same lot? *(objection-adjacent, ties to soil/access/season variables — same root cause as the homepage's cost-variance question, now scoped specifically to site prep)*
21. Does site prep for an ADU cost less than for a full house, since the footprint is smaller? *(answered, directionally: ADU-specific cost guides note site prep still runs a wide range depending on site complexity even at a smaller footprint — no flat "ADU discount" implied)*

## process

22. What are the actual steps in a site-prep job, in order? *(answered, synthesized from All Dirt/BBC/industry sources: survey → clear vegetation/debris → strip and stockpile topsoil → cut and fill to grade → compact subgrade → stake corners/elevations → rough-grade for drainage)*
23. How long does site preparation take for a typical residential lot? *(answered: a well-kept, already-open lot can be cleared in about a week; overall residential site prep commonly runs 1-2 months per the homepage pack's Horst Excavating sourcing — the range depends heavily on vegetation, soil, and weather)*
24. How long does site prep take for a commercial or subdivision project? *(answered: commercial site excavation commonly runs 1-3 weeks, longer for multi-building projects, per the homepage pack's sourcing)*
25. What equipment shows up for a residential site-prep job versus a commercial one?
26. Does the topsoil that gets stripped get reused, or hauled away?
27. What does "rough grading" mean versus "finish grading" — do I need both for site prep?
28. Is compaction testing required, or does the contractor just compact and move on?

## oregon_specific / permitting

29. Do I need a permit for site prep in Lane County? *(answered: depends on scope — Lane County's Grading and Fill Permit is triggered by moving more than 50 cubic yards of earth or a cut/fill slope steeper than 2:1, per a third-party guide; foundation excavation under an active building permit is generally exempt, but driveway/landscape grading is not — see `07-oregon-authority.md` §1)*
30. What's the difference between a grading permit and a building permit? *(answered directly in `07-oregon-authority.md` §1)*
31. Does clearing brush or trees on my own land require a permit in Lane County?
32. Do I need an erosion-control permit for site prep? *(oregon_specific — answered: Lane County's Erosion Prevention Permit has a Type I tier for single-family homes/minor grading under 1 acre, and a Type II tier for larger/steeper/more erodible sites, within the county's MS4 area — see `07-oregon-authority.md` §2)*
33. What is the DEQ 1200-C permit, and do I need it for my project? *(oregon_specific, high-value — answered in full in `07-oregon-authority.md` §4: required for 1+ acre of disturbance or a larger common development plan totaling 1+ acre; zero of 9 competitors mention this at all)*
34. Does clearing land for a subdivision or commercial site trigger different permit rules than a single house lot? *(oregon_specific — answered via the DEQ 1200-C "common plan of development" rule)*
35. If my septic system needs a test pit, does that have to happen before or after grading? *(oregon_specific — answered in `07-oregon-authority.md` §5: before, and the approved drainfield area must not be disturbed afterward)*
36. What's the best time of year to do site prep in Oregon's rainy climate? *(oregon_specific — reused finding from homepage pack: May-October is the standard dry-season recommendation; Willamette Valley clay makes wet-season work slower and costlier)*
37. Does Lane County require a survey before issuing a grading permit?

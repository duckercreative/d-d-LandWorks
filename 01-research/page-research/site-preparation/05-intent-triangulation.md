## Table 1 — Pain Points (specific to someone about to build, not general excavation-shopping)

| Pain point | Source | Severity | Addressed in current competitor content? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not knowing whether site prep happens before or after hiring a builder/GC | Construction-sequence literature (gocodes.com, prestigioushomebuilders.net) — this is a genuinely confusing sequencing question with no single universal answer | High | No — 0/9 competitors address sequencing at all |
| Fear that site prep will delay the building permit | Implied directly by the existence of separate grading-vs-building-permit tracks in Lane County (`07-oregon-authority.md` §1) | High | No |
| Fear of hitting rock, clay, or buried debris mid-job and blowing the budget | Cost-overrun literature (Angi, Rockzone Americas) — documented 10-40x cost jump for rock excavation | High | No — 0/9 competitors mention this possibility at all, let alone how they handle it |
| Confusion about whether "site prep" and "excavation" are the same service or different ones | Directly observed in competitor copy itself (Danco, Braun, Strasheim all list the two terms side-by-side without distinguishing them) | High | No — this is the core umbrella-hub gap, see `08-gap-analysis.md` |
| Not knowing if a survey is required before site work can start | Grading-industry sources (IBW Surveyors, Surf City Hydraulic) consistently note a survey precedes a grading plan | Medium-High | No |
| Worry that grading a lot for a house will interfere with or void a septic system's site evaluation | Lane County's own stated rule that disturbing an approved drainfield location can void approval (`07-oregon-authority.md` §5) | Medium-High (real regulatory consequence, not just inconvenience) | No — 0/9 competitors mention this sequencing risk |
| Uncertainty about whether their specific project (ADU, shop/barn, subdivision lot) needs a different permit tier than a standard house lot | DEQ 1200-C's "common plan of development" rule and Lane County's Type I/II erosion-permit split both scale with project type (`07-oregon-authority.md` §2, §4) | Medium | No |
| Feeling like "site preparation" is vague contractor-speak for "we'll figure it out and bill you" | General home-services trust pattern (same root cause the homepage pack documented for "we do everything" positioning) | Medium | No — 0/9 competitors itemize their process into a visible sequence |

## Table 3 — Trust Killers & Rebuttals (site-prep-specific)

| Trust killer | Why it matters here specifically | D&D rebuttal (evidence-based, no invented stats) |
|---|---|---|
| No competitor explains site prep vs. excavation as distinct services | A homeowner can't tell what they're actually buying, or whether they need one call or two | Open the page with a plain-language distinction: site prep is everything that gets a raw lot ready to build on (clearing, grading, compaction, staking); excavation is the specific digging for a foundation, utility line, or drainage feature that follows. State plainly that D&D handles the whole sequence under one contract. |
| Sequencing anxiety ("will this delay my permit?") | Zero competitors acknowledge that grading and building permits are separate tracks in Lane County | State the real distinction from `07-oregon-authority.md` §1: foundation excavation under an active building permit is generally exempt from a separate grading permit, but driveway/landscape grading is not — and recommend confirming specifics with Lane County LMD for the exact project |
| Fear of rock/clay cost overruns with no warning | Zero competitors mention this possibility, let alone offer a mitigation (soil study) | Explain honestly that Willamette Valley's clay soil and occasional rock can affect cost and timeline, and that a soil check before quoting protects the customer's budget rather than being an upsell |
| DEQ 1200-C / erosion permits never mentioned anywhere in this market | A customer planning a larger project (ADU on a big parcel, subdivision, commercial site) has no way to know this applies to them until they're already committed | Name the 1-acre threshold and the "common plan of development" rule plainly, with a clear "confirm your specific project with Lane County/DEQ" caveat — free authority-borrowing, zero-cost differentiation |
| Septic drainfield disturbance risk never mentioned | A homeowner grading their own lot (or hiring a low-cost operator) could unknowingly void a septic approval | State explicitly that D&D coordinates grading around an evaluated/approved drainfield location rather than treating the whole lot as one undifferentiated grading job |
| Vague process language ("we'll clear and grade it") | Reinforces the general contractor-distrust pattern already documented in the homepage pack | Publish the real, ordered process (survey/plan → clear → strip topsoil → cut/fill to grade → compact → stake) as a numbered sequence, not a bulleted feature list |

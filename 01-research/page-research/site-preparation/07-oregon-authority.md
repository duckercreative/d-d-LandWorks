## 1. Lane County Grading and Fill Permit (site-prep-specific — new for this page)

- **Trigger thresholds:** Per a 2026 third-party Lane County permitting guide (Willamette Craftworks), a Grading and Fill Permit is required when a project moves **more than 50 cubic yards of earth** or creates a **cut/fill slope steeper than 2:1**. **This is sourced from a secondary guide, not fetched directly from Lane Code Chapter 16 or the LMD's own permit page in this pass — flag as "verify exact cubic-yard/slope figures directly with Lane County LMD before publishing as a precise, quotable threshold," consistent with this project's no-fabrication rule.**
- **Foundation exemption:** Excavation performed specifically for a building foundation, under an active building permit, is generally exempt from a separate grading permit. Driveway and landscaping grading are **not** automatically exempt — this directly answers the "is site prep covered under my building permit or do I need something separate?" question this page needs to address.
- **Source:** [Willamette Craftworks — Lane County Building Permits: 2026 Homeowner's Guide](https://willamettecraftworks.com/permitting/lane-county-or/) · [Lane County Land Management Division](https://www.lanecounty.org/government/county_departments/public_works/land_management_division) · verified 2026-09-08.

## 2. Lane County Erosion Prevention Permit — Type I / Type II (new)

- **Type I:** Single-family homes or minor grading disturbing **under 1 acre**.
- **Type II:** Larger developments, slopes exceeding 15%, or highly erodible soils.
- **Where it applies:** Within the County's MS4 area — broadly, the Eugene/Springfield urban fringe where stormwater runoff drains to a regulated municipal system.
- **Why it matters for this page:** This is the local-level erosion-control permit that sits *below* the state-level DEQ 1200-C threshold (§4) — a project under 1 acre can still need a Type I erosion permit locally even when it doesn't trigger DEQ 1200-C. This two-tier structure (local Type I/II + state 1200-C) is worth explaining plainly, since no competitor page attempts it.
- **Source:** [Willamette Craftworks — Lane County Building Permits Guide](https://willamettecraftworks.com/permitting/lane-county-or/) · verified 2026-09-08. Same secondary-source caveat as §1 — confirm exact acreage/slope thresholds with Lane County LMD before publishing as exact figures.

## 4. DEQ 1200-C Construction Stormwater Permit — CONFIRMED DIRECTLY ON OREGON.GOV (new, and the single most important Oregon-specific fact for this page)

This is a real, currently-active Oregon DEQ program, verified directly against `oregon.gov/deq` (not a secondary source) — exactly the kind of fact the task brief asked to verify rather than assume:

- **What triggers it:** A construction project that **disturbs 1 acre or more of land**, OR is part of a **larger common plan of development that totals 1 acre or more** even if the individual lot itself is smaller (this "common plan" rule prevents a subdivision from being split into sub-1-acre parcels to dodge the permit — directly relevant to this page's subdivision/commercial project type).
- **What counts as "disturbance":** Clearing, grading, excavation, and similar earth-moving activities — i.e., exactly the work this page describes.
- **Timing:** A complete application must be submitted to DEQ (or its designated agent) **at least 30 calendar days before planned soil disturbance begins.**
- **Cost:** Approximately **$1,515 application fee** plus **$1,558 first-year annual fee**, plus a 4% technology fee, based on the current fee schedule found during this pass.
- **No homeowner exemption found:** This research pass found no stated exemption for single-family homeowners or simplified process for small residential lots once the 1-acre (or common-plan) threshold is met.
- **Permit term:** The current 1200-C general permit is effective **December 15, 2025 through December 14, 2030.**
- **Why this matters for the page:** Most homeowner-scale lots (a single house pad) will fall under 1 acre of disturbance and won't trigger this — but ADU projects on larger parcels, shop/barn pads with long access-road cuts, and any subdivision or commercial site-development project are realistic candidates. This is a genuine, differentiating piece of content: **zero of the 9 competitor pages reviewed for this page (see `04-competitor-matrix.md`) mention DEQ 1200-C at all.**
- **Source:** [Oregon DEQ — 1200-C Construction Stormwater Permit (Housing)](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/housing/Pages/housing-1200c.aspx) · [Oregon DEQ — 1200 Series Construction Stormwater Permits](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/wq/wqpermits/pages/stormwater-construction.aspx) · verified 2026-09-08.

## 5. Lane County On-Site Wastewater Program — Septic Test-Pit Sequencing (new)

- **Test-pit requirements:** A minimum of **2 test pits** (3 preferred), spaced **50-100 feet apart**, each **2 feet wide, 4 feet long, and 5 feet deep**, stepped or ramped on one end for safe inspector access.
- **Critical sequencing rule:** Once a drainfield location is proposed or approved, it must **not** be cut, filled, paved, have a well dug nearby, or otherwise be developed — doing so can **void the site's septic approval**. This is a direct, practical answer to "what happens if grading disturbs the area DEQ or the county evaluated for my septic system?" and a genuine reason a homeowner needs site-prep work sequenced *with*, not just *before*, a septic evaluation.
- **Why this matters for the page:** This page sits above `/septic/` in the site hierarchy per business-info.md's service list, and a site-prep contractor who understands this sequencing (evaluate soil → protect the drainfield footprint → grade everything else first) is a genuine trust/expertise signal that zero competitor site-prep pages currently demonstrate.
- **Source:** [Lane County — Test Pit Information](https://www.lanecounty.org/government/county_departments/public_works/land_management_division/on-_site_wastewater/test_pit_information) · [Lane County — On-Site Wastewater](https://www.lanecounty.org/government/county_departments/public_works/land_management_division/on-_site_wastewater) · verified 2026-09-08.

## 6. Rock/Clay Cost-Overrun Reality — Pacific Northwest-Specific (new)

- Standard excavation runs roughly **$5-$15 per cubic yard**; hitting solid rock can push that to **$50-$200 per cubic yard** (a 10-40× increase). Clay soil specifically is documented to add **25-50% to excavation costs**. Blasting or specialized rock-breaking equipment, when needed, can add **$10,000-$50,000+** to a job that looked straightforward on paper.
- **Prevention:** A geotechnical soil study (roughly **$2,000-$5,000**) on a difficult site is documented to typically prevent 20-50% of cost overruns — a genuine, honest answer to "what happens if you hit rock or bad soil partway through" that lets D&D frame a soil-check recommendation as protecting the customer's budget, not upselling.
- **PNW-specific corroboration:** A Pacific Northwest-focused industry guide (Straight Up Excavation, WA) independently confirms clay/rock/hillside excavation as a regionally distinct cost driver, consistent with the Willamette Valley clay-soil findings already documented in the homepage pack (`../home/07-oregon-authority.md` §5).
- **Source:** Aggregated from multiple national cost-guide sources (Angi, Rockzone Americas, RIB Software) cross-checked against [Straight Up Excavation & Drainage — Clay, Rock, and Hillside Excavation in the Pacific Northwest: 2026 Guide](https://www.straightupexcavationanddrainage.com/clay-rock-and-hillside-excavation-in-the-pacific-northwest-2026-guide) · verified 2026-09-08. Treat as directional industry ranges, not Lane-County-specific pricing (no Lane-County-specific rock/clay surcharge figure was found — do not present these ranges as D&D's own pricing).

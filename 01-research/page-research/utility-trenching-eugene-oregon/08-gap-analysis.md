## Top 5 Gaps (Ordered by Score)

### Gap 1 (tie) — Joint Trenching, Named and Explained (Score: 50.0) ← Highest
**What filling looks like:** A dedicated H2 section explaining what joint trenching is, when it's code-permitted in Lane County, what the separation rules are (water 12" above sewer at crossings), and why it saves money on new construction and ADU projects. With a concrete "here's what that looks like in one trench" explanation, D&D is immediately the most informative local source on this topic.
**David confirmation needed:** Whether D&D has performed joint trenching jobs.

### Gap 2 (tie) — ADU Utility Trenching, Explicitly Named (Score: 50.0) ← Highest
**What filling looks like:** ADU called out by name in the "project types" section. Oregon's SB 1051 and HB 2001 ADU-friendly laws briefly referenced to establish context. Every detached ADU needs water, sewer, gas, and electrical run to it — D&D handles this. High-demand, recurring, zero-competitor coverage.
**No David confirmation needed** — utility trenching for ADU connections is squarely within existing scope.

### Gap 3 (four-way tie at 40.0) — Burial Depth Table, Oregon811 Ticket Life, Permit-by-Scenario, Who Calls for the Tap

**Burial depth table:** The Lane County-specific per-utility depth table is the single most citable, most defensible content asset on this page. It's already verified in `07-oregon-authority.md`. Zero local Eugene competitors publish this.

**Oregon811 30-day ticket life:** The 2025/2026 OAR amendment reducing ticket life from 45 to 30 days is a live, differentiating regulatory detail. No competitor has updated their content with this change. Publishing it first makes D&D the current, authoritative local source.

**Permit-by-scenario:** The three-scenario breakdown (ROW Facility Permit, private property under building permit, grading threshold) answers the single most anxiety-producing question in the permit category without oversimplifying to "yes you need a permit."

**Who calls for the tap:** The disambiguation between OR811 (free locate notify — contractor calls this) and the utility company's tap application (property owner initiates, per general industry pattern) resolves the most common coordination confusion and directly gates calls.

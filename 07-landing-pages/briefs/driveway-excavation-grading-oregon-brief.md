#### H2-2: Why Do Gravel Driveways Fail in Oregon?
**Word count target:** 230 words
**Matrix A trace:** Drainage/failure topic — 3/8 competitors touch this superficially
**Gap trace:** GAPs 4, 5, 10 — failure-mode diagnosis + drainage-first framing (zero competitors do this explicitly)

Content requirements — name and explain all 5 failure modes:
1. **Gravel migration/washout** — no crown, no ditching, water channels down the surface
2. **Rutting** — base too thin OR clay punching through (no geotextile); tire tracks fill with water
3. **Washboard ripples** — surface instability under repeated traffic on clay-heavy base
4. **Soft spots / mud pumping** — saturated clay pumping up through base rock; classic sign of missing geotextile or inadequate base depth
5. **Edge erosion** — no shoulder grading; gravel falls off the sides

Lane County specificity: Malpass clay (Hydrologic Group D) loses load-bearing strength when saturated October through April. Geotextile fabric between clay subgrade and base rock is what separates a driveway that lasts from one that fails within two winters.

Trust-killer rebuttal: "Adding gravel to a driveway with bad drainage solves nothing permanently — the rock leaves with the next rain." Place at end of section.

Additional context:

Trust-killer rebuttal: "Contractors who skip geotextile on Lane County clay save $200–$400 on the install and cost you the driveway in two winters." Place in geotextile explanation.

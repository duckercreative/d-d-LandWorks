### H2: Who Calls 811 — and Who Calls the Utility Company?

**Format:** Short comparison note — two different calls, two different parties  
**Answer-first opener:**  
"Oregon811 and your utility company are two separate calls for two different purposes. Don't confuse them."

**Two-column explanation:**

**Call 1: Oregon 811 (Oregon Utility Notification Center)**  
- What it is: The free, state-mandated locate service — utility companies mark underground line locations with paint and flags before you dig
- Who calls: Both the homeowner and the excavation contractor are required to notify under ORS Chapter 757
- When: At least 2 business days before any digging begins; no more than 10 days in advance
- How: Call 811 (toll-free: 1-800-332-2344) or submit online at digsafelyoregon.com — free, 24/7
- Ticket life: **30 days** (reduced from 45 days in 2025/2026 under OAR 952-001-0010(23))
- What it covers: Public utility lines to the meter. **Does NOT cover private lines** (irrigation systems, outbuilding power, shed electrical) — those are the property owner's responsibility to locate
- D&D calls Oregon 811 on every project as a standard step — no exceptions

**Call 2: The utility company (EWEB, Springfield Utility Board, NW Natural)**  
- What it is: A service application to the utility company to initiate a new connection at the main or meter
- Who calls: Typically the property owner initiates the service application
- When: Before the trench is dug — the utility company may need to schedule a tap or meter placement
- EWEB: (541) 685-7000 | eweb.org
- Springfield Utility Board: (541) 746-8451 | subutil.com

### H2: What Permits Does Utility Trenching Require in Lane County?

**Format:** Numbered list — 3 scenarios  
**Answer-first opener:**  
"Whether you need a permit depends on where the trench runs, not just what utility it's for. Lane County uses three different triggers."

**Scenario 1 — Trench in the public right-of-way (ROW):**  
A Lane County **Facility Permit** is required from Lane County Public Works before work begins. This covers any pipe, conduit, or vault installed in a county road right-of-way. Application is free; permit fee equals actual processing cost (determined by staff, due before permit is issued). Processing time: **6–8 weeks** — factor this into your project timeline. Contact: 3050 N. Delta Hwy, Eugene, OR 97408 | (541) 682-6902.

**Scenario 2 — Trench entirely on private property, tied to a permitted structure:**  
Utility trenching associated with a building permit (new construction, ADU, addition) is typically covered under the associated plumbing, mechanical, or electrical permit. No separate trenching permit required.

**Scenario 3 — Grading scope trigger:**  
If the utility trench work involves moving **more than 50 cubic yards of earth** or creating a cut or fill slope steeper than 2:1, a separate grading/fill permit may be required. Most standard residential utility trenches are well under this threshold.

### H2: Eugene Soil and Wet-Season Timing for Utility Trenching

**Format:** 2-paragraph narrative — anti-doorway Eugene-specific content  
**Paragraph 1 — Soil:**  
Lane County's Willamette Valley floor is dominated by Malpass silty clay loam — a dense, moisture-retaining soil that affects excavation performance. In dry conditions (May through September), it cuts cleanly and compacts well. During Oregon's wet season (October through April), Malpass clay becomes saturated and significantly harder to work with — open trenches can fill with water, and compacting saturated backfill is difficult without proper dewatering. This is a reality that every Lane County excavation contractor manages, not a D&D-specific limitation.

**Paragraph 2 — Seasonal scheduling:**  
The best time to schedule a utility trenching project in Eugene is May through September. Wet-season trenching is possible for urgent repairs (a broken water line doesn't wait for May), but timeline, cost, and surface restoration outcomes are better in the dry window. If you're planning an ADU, new shop, or elective utility upgrade, calling in spring gives you the full dry-season window and avoids the scheduling backlog that typically develops in May and June.

### H2: Frequently Asked Questions

**Format:** FAQPage schema — 10 questions  
**All answers should be answer-first, 40–80 words each.**

**Q1: How deep does a water service line need to be buried in Lane County, Oregon?**  
Water service lines must be buried at least 24 inches below grade in Lane County — 12 inches below the local 12-inch frost depth, as required by Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code 609.1. Deeper burial may be required under driveways, parking areas, or where the inspector specifies.

**Q2: How deep does a sewer lateral need to be buried?**  
Sanitary sewer laterals need at least 12 inches of cover above the pipe in Lane County (OPSC 718.1). The pipe must also slope at least ¼ inch per foot toward the sewer main or septic tank to ensure gravity drainage.

**Q3: How deep does electrical conduit need to be buried in Oregon?**  
In Oregon, PVC electrical conduit must be at least 18 inches deep in open ground and 24 inches deep under driveways or parking areas, per NEC Table 300.5 (adopted by Oregon's Electrical Specialty Code). Rigid metal conduit can be as shallow as 6 inches.

**Q4: How deep does a gas line need to be buried in Oregon?**  
Standard residential gas piping must be at least 18 inches below grade in Oregon (Oregon Residential Specialty Code G2415.12). Lines feeding outdoor portable appliances — generators, BBQ drops, outdoor heaters — can be as shallow as 8 inches under ORSC G2415.12.1.

**Q5: Can water, sewer, electrical, and gas all go in the same trench?**  
Yes — joint trenching is code-permitted in Lane County with modern pipe materials. Water must be positioned at least 12 inches above the sewer line where they cross (OPSC 720.1). Utility companies (EWEB, NW Natural) may have their own separation specs beyond code minimums — confirm these when you initiate your service application.

**Q6: Who is required to call Oregon 811 before utility trenching?**  
Under ORS Chapter 757, both the property owner and the excavation contractor are legally required to notify Oregon 811 at least two business days before any digging begins. The service is free — call 811 or 1-800-332-2344, or submit online at digsafelyoregon.com. D&D calls Oregon 811 on every project.

**Q7: How long is a utility locate ticket good for in Oregon?**  
As of 2025/2026, Oregon 811 locate tickets are valid for 30 days — reduced from the previous 45-day window under an amendment to OAR 952-001-0010(23). For projects that run longer than 30 days, Oregon 811 must be notified again before the ticket expires.

**Q8: Do I need a permit for utility trenching in Lane County?**  
It depends on where the trench runs. Work in a Lane County public right-of-way requires a Facility Permit from Lane County Public Works (6–8 weeks processing, (541) 682-6902). Work on private property tied to a building permit is typically covered under that permit. A grading permit is triggered separately at 50+ cubic yards of earth moved.

**Q9: Does trenching under a driveway or concrete cost more than open ground?**  
Yes. Cutting, removing, and patching concrete or asphalt adds meaningful labor and material cost on top of the base trench price. If your trench crosses a driveway, patio, or parking area, mention it when you call for an estimate — it affects the scope significantly.

**Q10: What is the difference between utility trenching and utility excavation?**  
Utility trenching is a specific type of utility excavation — a narrow trench for running a service line (water, sewer, gas, electrical) to a structure. Utility excavation is the broader category that also includes locating existing lines, excavating around buried infrastructure, and coordination with utility companies. See our [utility excavation services page](/utility-excavation/) for the full scope.

## 8. FAQ Section

All 8 questions sourced from `02-question-mine.md`. Each traces to a specific category. Schema-ready — copy these Q&A pairs exactly into FAQPage schema.

**Q1 (local_specific / objection):** Do I need a permit to grade my property in Lane County?  
**A:** Yes, if your project moves more than 50 cubic yards of earth or creates a cut or fill slope steeper than 2:1. Contact Lane County Land Management Division at 3050 N. Delta Hwy, Eugene (M–F 9am–3pm). Foundation excavation under an active building permit is generally exempt; driveway and landscaping grading is not. We handle permit guidance for Lane County projects — call before you start and we'll tell you what applies to your project.

**Q2 (cost):** How much does land grading cost in Eugene, Oregon?  
**A:** National cost ranges are $0.08–$2.00 per square foot for yard grading, $1,000–$5,125 for driveway grading, and $1,000–$6,700 for foundation prep. Eugene area projects vary based on lot size, soil type, slope, and whether fill material is needed. Malpass clay soil can add time to projects — we'll flag that during the site visit. Contact D&D Land Works at 541-401-8726 for a free, itemized estimate with no obligation.

**Q3 (process / pre_call):** What is the difference between rough grading and finish grading?  
**A:** Rough grading is the bulk earthmoving stage — we set the building pad elevation, strip topsoil, and rough in drainage falls, typically within ±0.1 foot of the plan. Finish grading follows after underground utilities are installed; it refines the surface to ±0.5 inch precision and prepares the site for paving, planting, or final inspection. Both stages can be done by D&D for new construction in Lane County.

**Q4 (local_specific / objection):** Do you call Oregon 811 before starting grading work?  
**A:** Yes, always. Oregon law requires contractors to notify Oregon 811 (the Utility Notification Center) at least two business days before any excavation or grading begins. We submit the locate request as a standard part of every job — before equipment touches the ground. Underground lines are marked by the utility companies, and we verify the marks before we start. This protects your property and is required by Oregon OSHA.

**Q5 (process / decision):** Can grading fix standing water in my Eugene yard?  
**A:** Often yes. Water pooling in Willamette Valley yards is frequently caused by flat or reverse-sloped grade — particularly where Malpass clay soil reduces drainage. We can regrade to direct water away from your home and toward proper outlets. On sites with heavy clay where slope correction alone won't drain fast enough, we may recommend combining regrading with a French drain or drainage swale for complete resolution. A free site visit is the best way to assess your specific conditions.

**Q6 (local_specific / process):** When is the best time to schedule grading in Oregon?  
**A:** May through September is ideal — dry conditions reduce erosion risk and keep project costs lower. Grading can be done October through April but Oregon's wet season (Oct 1 – Apr 30) requires additional erosion control measures under state law, which adds time and material cost. Wet season work also depends on how saturated the soil is — Malpass clay stays workable longer than some soils but won't tolerate heavy equipment when waterlogged. Contact us early; spring and summer slots fill quickly.

**Q7 (objection):** How do I verify D&D Land Works' contractor license?  
**A:** D&D Land Works holds CCB License #261742 with the Oregon Construction Contractors Board. You can verify our active license status, bond, and insurance at orccb.org. Look for both an Active license status and a current bond — a license can show Active even if the bond has separately lapsed. We're also DEQ Certified for septic work. Licensed and bonded from the start — not after something goes wrong.

**Q8 (pre_call / decision):** What is a building pad and do I need one?  
**A:** A building pad is a leveled, compacted ground platform at a precise engineered elevation — required before any structure (home, shop, barn, ADU) can be built on raw land. It's different from general yard leveling: a pad must meet specific elevation specs set by your architect or engineer, including bearing capacity standards. D&D grades building pads for residential homes, outbuildings, and commercial structures across Lane County. We work from your engineered drawings or can help you figure out what you need.

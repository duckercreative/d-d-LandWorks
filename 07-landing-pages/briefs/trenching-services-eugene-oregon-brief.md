## 8. FAQ Section

All 10 questions sourced from `02-question-mine.md` FAQ shortlist. FAQPage schema deploys all 10.

**Q1: How deep do irrigation lines need to be buried in Lane County, Oregon?**
A: Irrigation mainlines in Lane County are typically buried 6 to 8 inches deep; lateral lines 4 to 6 inches. Oregon has no state code minimum for irrigation line burial depth — unlike water service lines, which must be 24 inches deep per Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code Section 609.1. In Willamette Valley clay, the practical depth driver is root intrusion from established landscaping, not frost.

**Q2: What's the difference between a sprinkler mainline trench and a lateral trench?**
A: The mainline is the primary supply line running from the backflow preventer or zone valve to the distribution points — it's usually larger diameter and carries more pressure. Laterals are the smaller branch lines running to individual heads. Mainlines are buried deeper (6–8 inches) to protect them from mechanical damage; laterals can be shallower (4–6 inches) since they carry less pressure and are typically in garden beds or turf.

**Q3: How much does trenching cost in Eugene, Oregon?**
A: Standard trenching in open dirt in Lane County typically runs $12–$25 per linear foot. Rocky or hard-pan soil is $30–$60+ per foot. Irrigation-specific trenching (smaller trench, tighter tolerances) runs $8–$40+ per linear foot for the trench portion. Mobilization minimum is typically $500–$1,500+. Call 541-401-8726 for a free estimate on your project.

**Q4: How much does it cost to trench 100 feet for irrigation?**
A: A 100-foot irrigation trench in Lane County typically falls in the $1,200–$2,500 range, including trench, backfill, and compaction — not including pipe, fittings, or the irrigation system components. Factors that move the price: soil conditions, whether the trench crosses any hardscape, and access to the site.

**Q5: Do I need to call Oregon 811 before digging an irrigation trench?**
A: Yes. Oregon law (ORS 757.545) requires anyone excavating — including irrigation trenching on private property — to notify Oregon 811 at least 2 business days before digging. Call 811 or submit online at oregon811.org. It's free. Marked utilities protect your project and your neighbors' service lines. D&D calls 811 before every project as a standard step.

**Q6: Do I need a permit to dig an irrigation trench in Lane County?**
A: For most residential irrigation trenches on private property that don't cross a county road right-of-way, no permit is required in Lane County. If the trench crosses a county road, a Lane County Facility Permit is needed. Projects that move more than 50 cubic yards of material may trigger a grading review. We'll flag any permit requirements at the estimate stage.

**Q7: Is trenching the same as ditch digging?**
A: They're often used interchangeably, but "trenching" typically refers to a narrow, deep cut for utilities or pipe, while "ditch digging" usually means a wider, open channel for drainage or water conveyance. D&D handles both — narrow irrigation and utility trenches as well as wider drainage ditches for rural and residential properties in Lane County.

**Q8: Can you trench under a driveway, or do you need to bore?**
A: We can open-cut a driveway trench and restore the surface, but for most homeowners, directional boring (trenchless) is less disruptive for crossing a paved driveway — it avoids breaking out and repouring asphalt or concrete. D&D can discuss which method fits your project. For short crossings under an unpaved driveway, open-cut is usually faster and lower cost.

**Q9: Can I DIY my irrigation trench, or should I hire a contractor?**
A: For a short, shallow run (under 50 feet, no utilities known nearby, open dirt), DIY with a rented walk-behind trencher is reasonable. For runs over 50 feet, clay soil in wet season, trench near utilities, or where hardscape crossing is involved, a professional saves time and prevents compaction problems. Either way: call Oregon 811 first before any digging.

**Q10: Do you serve Springfield and other Lane County cities beyond Eugene?**
A: Yes. D&D serves Eugene, Springfield, Junction City, Cottage Grove, Creswell, Veneta, Coburg, and surrounding Lane County communities. We also serve Florence, Oakridge, and Lowell. Call 541-401-8726 to confirm service to your specific address.

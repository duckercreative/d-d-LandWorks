## What excavation work covers

Excavation is earthmoving for a specific purpose: digging to a depth or dimension a project requires, moving material somewhere else, or getting material off a site entirely. Here's what falls under excavation at D&D:

- **Foundation excavation** — digging to the required depth and footprint for a slab, crawl space, or basement
- **Utility trenching** — cutting for water lines, sewer laterals, electrical conduit, or drain field runs; Oregon 811 locates are required before any trench is opened
- **Site preparation excavation** — bulk earthmoving to bring a site to the rough grade a building project needs before work begins
- **Backfill and compaction** — returning and compacting material in measured lifts after footings, pipes, or conduit are set; uncompacted backfill settles unevenly and causes structural problems years later
- **Rock removal** — breaking out and hauling off material the bucket can't move by force alone
- **Drainage excavation** — cutting swales, French drains, curtain drains, retention basins, and outlet channels to manage water on a site
- **Pond and building pad excavation** — farm ponds, decorative ponds, and level pads for shops, barns, and accessory structures
- **Driveway and access road cutting** — grading and cutting stable vehicle access into undeveloped parcels, or repairing failed subgrade on existing driveways

## What's the difference between excavation and grading?

Excavation moves material. It's the vertical work, the cut, the hole. Grading shapes a surface to a precise elevation or slope. It's the horizontal work, the final geometry. On most real jobs they happen in the same operation: excavation opens the cut or moves the mass, grading finishes the surface to design grade. When a homeowner needs standalone re-sloping or yard leveling for drainage without any real digging, that's usually closer to [site preparation work](/site-preparation-contractor-eugene-oregon). D&D scopes the job by walking the site, so if a call turns out to need grading rather than excavation, we'll say so.

## Eugene's soil and seasons — what it means for your project

Lane County's valley floor is dominated by Malpass silty clay loam, a clay-heavy soil series documented in the USDA's Web Soil Survey for this part of western Oregon. Malpass clay holds moisture well into spring. In wet conditions, heavy equipment leaves ruts and getting proper compaction is difficult, sometimes impractical. In dry summer conditions, the same clay can be harder than expected for a small machine — not rock, but not easy digging either. None of this is unusual; it's just what Lane County's valley floor does, and a contractor who works here regularly knows to build it into the scope.

Oregon's wet season runs October through April in Lane County. Most residential excavation projects — foundation digs, pond construction, access roads, building pads — are easier to schedule before October or after April when the ground is workable. Some jobs can't wait, and D&D has worked through Lane County's wet months. If there's scheduling flexibility, we'll flag it on the estimate walk. If there isn't, we'll scope the job to account for the conditions. Wet-season timing is part of the estimate conversation up front, not a conversation that happens after work has started.

## Do we call before digging? Yes — Oregon 811 on every job

Oregon law requires calling Oregon 811, the Oregon Utility Notification Center, at least two business days before any excavation. The call is free. Utility companies then send locators to mark underground lines with color-coded paint: gas, water, electric, fiber, telecom. D&D calls Oregon 811 on every job. That's what protects a homeowner from a utility-strike situation, and it's what protects D&D. The Oregon Public Utility Commission enforces penalties for excavators who skip the locate. ## Do you need a permit to excavate in Lane County?

Lane County's Grading and Fill Permit is generally triggered by moving 50 or more cubic yards, or by a cut or fill slope steeper than 2:1. The permit is issued by Lane County's Land Management Division. Foundation excavation under an active building permit is typically exempt from a separate grading permit, but driveway, access road, and landscaping excavation are not automatically exempt.

Requirements vary by parcel, scope, and zoning. D&D can help identify what's likely required during the estimate walk. For your specific project, confirm with Lane County LMD directly. For a full breakdown of excavation and site prep permits in Lane County, see our [site preparation services page](/site-preparation-contractor-eugene-oregon).

*The 50 cubic yard threshold comes from a third-party permitting guide, not directly from Lane Code Chapter 16. Confirm with LMD before treating it as an exact legal threshold for your project.*

If a project needs one of those, D&D Land Works says so upfront instead of quietly subcontracting it out.

Utility excavation and trenching work follows OSHA's excavation standard, 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P: trenches five feet deep or more require a protective system such as shoring or sloping, and any excavation four feet deep or more needs safe access (a ladder, steps, or ramp) within 25 feet of lateral travel. A "competent person," an OSHA term for someone trained and authorized to identify site hazards, is involved on anything shallower than that threshold too. That's the real federal standard D&D Land Works works under, not an internal policy.

## What Does DEQ Certified Mean for Septic Work?

"DEQ Certified" means D&D Land Works holds a certification from the Oregon Department of Environmental Quality that's required to legally construct, install, or repair septic systems in the state. Septic work in Oregon is governed by state rules under Oregon Administrative Rules Chapter 340, Division 071, and DEQ maintains the installer certification that authorizes someone to do this work. Without it, doing septic work in Oregon isn't legal, full stop. In septic work generally, if repairing a failing system would cost more than about half of a full replacement, replacement is usually the smarter long-term call. D&D Land Works will tell you honestly which side of that line your project falls on, instead of steering you toward whichever job pays more.

## How Much Does Excavation or Site Prep Cost in Lane County?

There isn't an honest single number for this. The cost of an excavation, grading, or site-prep job in Lane County depends on how big the area is, how the site is accessed, what's already on the lot, and what the soil and slope look like once equipment is on-site. Instead of publishing a generic dollar figure that would be wrong for most projects, D&D Land Works gives every project a real, no-obligation estimate after seeing the site.

What actually moves the number on a given job:

| Factor | What it affects |
|---|---|
| Project scope | A single driveway regrade costs less than a full building-pad site prep — scope drives most of the variation |
| Site access | Tight, hard-to-reach lots or long haul distances for equipment and material add time and cost |
| Soil and slope conditions | Willamette Valley clay soil and steep or unstable slopes can require extra passes, rock, or erosion control |
| Season | Work scheduled in the wet season (roughly November–April) often needs more rock and drainage work than the same job done in the dry season |
| Permits | Projects that require a Lane County or city permit add review time and fees on top of the excavation itself |
| Utility/septic involvement | Adding utility trenching or septic work to a grading job changes both timeline and cost |

## When Is the Best Time of Year to Excavate in Oregon?

May through October is the standard dry-season window for excavation and grading work in the Willamette Valley, and it's the window D&D Land Works recommends when a project's timeline allows for it. Eugene averages 40.83 inches of rain a year, with December (7.21 in.), January (6.05 in.), and November (5.98 in.) as the wettest months on record. Willamette Valley clay soil holds water poorly. It hardens when dry but turns sticky and rutted under equipment when wet, and it won't compact reliably in that state. Wet-season work (roughly November through April) is still workable, and D&D Land Works does it regularly, but it typically calls for extra rock, tighter erosion control, and a bit more schedule flexibility than the same job would need in July.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does "DEQ Certified" mean, and why does it matter for septic work?**
DEQ Installer Certification is the credential Oregon requires to legally construct, install, or repair a septic system. It's issued and enforced by the Oregon Department of Environmental Quality under Oregon Administrative Rules Chapter 340, Division 071. It's not a marketing badge — it's the legal basis for doing septic work in this state at all, and D&D Land Works holds it.

**How do I verify D&D Land Works' Oregon CCB license?**
Search CCB #261742 directly at the Oregon Construction Contractors Board's [official license lookup](https://search.ccb.state.or.us/search/). You don't need to take D&D Land Works' word for it — the record is public.

**Can one contractor really handle site prep, grading, drainage, and septic?**
Yes. D&D Land Works covers site preparation, land clearing, grading and leveling, drainage excavation, utility excavation, and DEQ-certified septic installation and repair under one Oregon CCB license, so a project that touches several of those doesn't need to be split across separate contractors.

**How much does excavation or site prep typically cost in Lane County?**
It depends too much on scope, access, soil, and season to give an honest number without seeing the site — a small driveway regrade and a full building-pad site prep aren't comparable jobs. D&D Land Works provides a free, written estimate after an on-site visit rather than a generic number that wouldn't apply to your project anyway.

**What is the best time of year to excavate in Oregon's rainy climate?**
May through October is the standard dry-season window for excavation and grading in the Willamette Valley. Wet-season work (November–April) is still doable and D&D Land Works does it, but it usually needs extra rock and erosion control and can move slower than the same job in summer.

**Do I need a permit to grade my land or clear brush in Lane County?**
It depends on the project. Foundation excavation done under an active building permit is generally exempt from a separate grading permit, but driveway and access work connecting to a county road usually isn't. Lane County's Land Management Division handles these determinations — confirm your specific project's permit status with them or ask D&D Land Works for guidance during your estimate.

**How long does site preparation take before construction can start?**
Residential site preparation typically runs one to two months from start to a builder-ready lot, though the excavation itself is often just a matter of days once the project is scoped, permitted, and scheduled. Larger or commercial projects usually take longer depending on scope and permitting.

**What should I ask before hiring any excavation contractor?**
Ask to see their Oregon CCB license number and verify it yourself at search.ccb.state.or.us, ask for a written estimate rather than a verbal number, and ask for a realistic timeline before you sign anything. A contractor who's comfortable with all three of those questions is generally a safer bet than one who isn't.

**Does D&D Land Works serve my city in Lane County?**
Almost certainly. D&D Land Works serves Eugene and Springfield as its primary service areas, plus Cottage Grove, Junction City, Creswell, Veneta, Florence, Oakridge, Coburg, and Lowell. See [every Lane County city D&D Land Works serves](/service-areas/) or call 541-401-8726 to confirm your address.

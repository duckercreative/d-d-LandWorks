## What Does Foundation Excavation Include?

Foundation excavation covers the below-grade work from topsoil strip to rough perimeter grade. For a typical Lane County residential project, the scope runs from Oregon 811 utility locate through haul-off of excavated material. D&D coordinates around the building department's footing inspection schedule so the sequence from excavation to concrete pour stays on the GC's timeline, not held up by a missed inspection window.

Oregon Residential Specialty Code (ORSC R401.3) requires the grade at the foundation perimeter to fall at least 6 inches within the first 10 feet away from the building. D&D accounts for this in the rough perimeter grading at the close of excavation.

Foundation excavation scope for a standard Lane County residential job:

- Oregon 811 utility locate, submitted at least 2 business days before any digging begins (digsafelyoregon.com; Oregon law requires it on every project)
- Organic topsoil strip from the building footprint
- Excavation to footing depth (12-inch minimum per ORSC R403.1.4; deeper if Willamette Valley clay requires over-excavation to reach firm bearing soil)
- Over-dig working space around the foundation perimeter (2-foot minimum width for forms, waterproofing, and working access)
- Haul-off of excavated material (wet clay in Lane County runs heavier than dry material, particularly in October through May)
- Rough grading of the excavation perimeter per ORSC R401.3 grade-fall requirements
- Coordination with the building department for footing inspection before concrete is placed

## Which Foundation Type Requires What Excavation?

The foundation type determines how much material moves, how long the job takes, and how many truck loads haul off-site. A slab, a crawl space, and a full basement are three different excavation scopes. The table below covers the practical breakdown for Lane County residential construction.

Foundation excavation scope by foundation type in Eugene and Lane County, Oregon:

| Foundation Type | Excavation Scope | Typical Depth | Est. Time | Approx. Material Volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Slab-on-grade | Topsoil strip + perimeter footing trench | 2 to 3 ft | 0.5 to 1 day | 30 to 80 cu yd |
| Crawl space | Perimeter trench to footing depth + stem wall working space | 2 to 4 ft | 0.75 to 1.5 days | 50 to 150 cu yd |
| Full basement | Full-footprint excavation, shoring or benching, haul | 8 to 10 ft | 2 to 3 days | 200 to 500 cu yd |

Crawl space foundations are the most common type in Eugene and Lane County, particularly in homes built before 2000, according to local Lane County real estate data. Full basements are the least common: Oregon's mild winters reduce the seasonal demand for basement space, and west Eugene's high winter water table complicates deep excavation and drainage requirements.

For foundation drainage: Willamette Valley clay soils are not Group I (well-drained) soils under the Unified Soil Classification System, which means ORSC R405 foundation drain requirements apply to basements and enclosed crawlspaces throughout most of Lane County. For full detail on foundation drainage and over-dig requirements, see [foundation excavation requirements under Oregon code](/foundation-excavation/).

## How Deep Do Footings Need to Be in Eugene?

In Lane County, exterior footings must be placed at least 12 inches below finished grade on undisturbed soil, per Oregon Residential Specialty Code R403.1.4. Lane County sits at approximately 430 feet elevation, well below the 2,500-foot threshold in ORSC Table R301.2(1), so the frost protection depth for the entire Lane County valley floor is also 12 inches. That single number governs both requirements for most Eugene-area foundations.

Oregon Residential Specialty Code R403.1.4 states: "Exterior footings shall be placed not less than 12 inches (305 mm) below the finished grade on undisturbed ground surface."

The 12-inch minimum applies across all of the Lane County valley: Eugene, Springfield, Cottage Grove, Junction City, Creswell, Veneta, and Florence all sit in the same elevation band. Contractors working in the Cascades foothills or at higher elevation should verify the applicable frost depth for that specific location against ORSC Table R301.2(1).

One exception matters in Eugene specifically. Saturated Malpass clay at the 12-inch depth during wet season (October through May) can be too soft to provide adequate bearing capacity for a footing load. In those conditions, D&D over-excavates to 14, 16, or 18 inches to reach dry, firm bearing soil. The code sets the minimum; the soil sets the actual depth. A site assessment before quoting accounts for this.

## What Is Eugene's Soil Like for Foundation Excavation?

Eugene's soil context is one of the most misunderstood factors homeowners and GCs bring to a foundation job. National excavation content focuses on rock as the primary cost risk. In Eugene, rock is not the issue. Clay is.

Malpass clay, documented in BLM Technical Note 447, is the dominant soil series in west Eugene. It has high shrink-swell properties and very low permeability. In wet season, saturated Malpass clay can form a heavy clay horizon starting at approximately 19 inches depth in some Eugene profiles. A footing bearing on saturated Malpass clay at 12 inches is bearing on unstable material. D&D assesses soil conditions at the site before any quote is finalized.

Unlike Central Oregon, where volcanic basalt sits close to the surface and rock ripping or blasting is a real budget line, the Willamette Valley floor has no significant bedrock. The Missoula Floods deposited deep clay and silt across the valley over thousands of years. Rock excavation cost premiums that appear in national content do not apply to most Lane County foundation jobs. Clay behavior, not rock, is the variable D&D plans around.

The wet season runs October through May in the Willamette Valley, a 7-month window during which saturated soils and perched groundwater can affect compaction performance and footing bearing. D&D excavates year-round. Wet-season work in Lane County adds erosion control measures required for sites over 500 square feet under Lane County LMD rules and may require dewatering in low-lying areas, particularly in west Eugene and along the valley floor. These are standard Lane County field practices. They are not reasons to delay a project with a fixed builder timeline.

## Who Schedules the Footing Inspection in Lane County?

The footing inspection happens after excavation, forms, and rebar are placed, and before concrete is poured. Who schedules it depends on where the project sits: inside Eugene city limits, or in unincorporated Lane County.

**City of Eugene permits:** Schedule through the eBuild system at pdd.eugene-or.gov/ebuild, or call City of Eugene Building Inspection Services at 541-682-5283. Inspection support line hours run 7:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., Monday through Friday. Requests placed before 7 a.m. are eligible for same-day inspection. The City of Eugene Building Inspection office is located at the Atrium Building, 99 W. 10th Ave., Suite 230, Eugene.

**Unincorporated Lane County permits:** Contact Lane County Building Safety at 541-682-4651. At least 48 hours of advance notice is required before a footing inspection. Lane County Land Management Division is located at 3050 N. Delta Hwy., Eugene.

The inspection sequence for a standard residential foundation in Lane County:

1. Excavation complete. Footing depth and over-dig working space established.
2. General contractor places footing forms and rebar (UFER grounding electrode is also placed at this stage for electrical co-inspection).
3. D&D or the GC schedules the footing inspection with the appropriate jurisdiction.
4. Building official inspects the open excavation. All work must be visible; no concrete placed yet.
5. Inspection passes. Concrete pour proceeds.

D&D coordinates the inspection schedule with the GC and the building department. A missed inspection window does not have to mean a lost day.

## How Does Foundation Excavation Work for an ADU?

Eugene's ADU activity has grown significantly following ORS 197.312, which removed local barriers to ADU construction in Oregon. Most ADU foundations in Lane County are crawl space or slab; the foundation type depends on the builder's specification and the site conditions.

ADU foundation excavation follows the same Oregon Residential Specialty Code frost-depth requirements as primary dwelling construction: 12-inch minimum footing depth, per ORSC R403.1.4. Foundation drainage under ORSC R405 applies to ADU basements and enclosed crawlspaces in Lane County clay soils the same as it does to the primary residence.

One distinction applies when the ADU excavation happens near or adjacent to an existing structure. OSHA 1926.652(b) requires that any excavation below the base of an existing footing be protected by underpinning or shoring, or that a licensed engineer certify no excavation hazard exists. This requirement is not optional and cannot be waived by a homeowner or GC on-site. D&D accounts for the underpinning requirement in the site assessment for every ADU dig that involves excavating near an existing foundation.

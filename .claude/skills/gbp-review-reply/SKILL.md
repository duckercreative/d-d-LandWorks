# GBP Review Reply — 75 Degree AC

## Why This Skill Exists

Google reply quality has become a ranking signal. SpamBrain (post-August 2025 update) now
detects template patterns across review responses. AI-generated or copy-paste replies hurt
trust with future readers AND can suppress GBP visibility. Every reply must read like a real
person at the company wrote it.

---

## The Core Rules (Non-Negotiable)

### Length
- **2–4 sentences. 40–80 words. Hard ceiling: 100 words.**
- Positive reviews need warmth, not volume. Long replies signal insecurity.
- If the review is one line ("Great job"), two sentences back is enough.
- If the review is detailed and enthusiastic, a fuller reply (3–4 sentences) is appropriate.

### Response Time
- Positive 5-star: within 24–48 hours
- Mixed/4-star: within 24 hours
- Negative/complaint: within 4–24 hours

### Structure (positive reviews)
1. **Name + specific echo** — reference the exact thing they praised (1 sentence)
2. **Reinforce their experience** — validate what they noticed, add one human touch (1 sentence)
3. **Forward-looking close** — soft, warm, never salesy (1 sentence)

---

## Voice & Tone

**Target voice:** Friendly, competent, local. Like a professional who actually cares — not a
customer service bot, not a car dealership, not a law firm.

- Use contractions: "we're" not "we are", "it's" not "it is"
- Vary sentence length — one short punchy line + one fuller line sounds human
- Mild conversational filler is fine, once: "Honestly, that made our day" / "That means a lot
  to the whole crew" / "That's really great to hear"
- Match the reviewer's energy. Enthusiastic review → slightly warmer reply. Terse review →
  brief, confident reply.
- Houston-appropriate: casual-warm, never stiff corporate

---

## Banned Patterns (Hard Block — Never Use)

These are AI tells and template signals. Using any of these will get flagged by SpamBrain
and will read as robotic to future prospects.

**Banned openers:**
- "Thank you for your valuable feedback"
- "Thank you for your kind words"
- "We appreciate your positive feedback"
- "We're so glad you had a great experience"
- Any opener that starts with "We" — start with the reviewer's name or a reaction

**Banned phrases anywhere in the reply:**
- "valuable feedback"
- "great experience" (overused — 80%+ of AI replies use this exact phrase)
- "valued customer"
- "we strive to provide"
- "absolutely thrilled" / "so honored" / "incredibly grateful"
- "outstanding service" / "exceptional service" (self-congratulatory)
- "our team of experts"
- "don't hesitate to contact us"
- "we look forward to serving you again" (robotic close)

**Banned structural patterns:**
- Identical reply to two reviews in the same week
- Same opener word/phrase more than twice in seven days
- Forcing a keyword + city into every reply
- Phone number in a positive review reply (GBP policy + feels promotional)
- Website URL in a positive review reply
- Any promotional offer, discount, or CTA

---

## Opener Rotation

Never start with the same word twice in a week. Rotate across these openers:

| Opener Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Name + reaction | "Natalie, so glad we could get to you fast!" |
| Name + echo | "Daniel, Kenny's going to love seeing his name come up!" |
| Exclamation on their word | "On-time AND done quickly — that's exactly what we aim for, Zoe." |
| Emotional beat | "Minh, 'as always' — that's the line that hits different." |
| Short punchy statement | "Really happy to hear this, Maria." |
| Their specific detail first | "A neat job AND a clear explanation — that's the combo, Lino." |

---

## Keyword & Location Usage

**Yes, but once, naturally — not as a formula.**

- Service keyword: embed in how a real person would say it. "Glad the AC repair went smoothly"
  not "Thank you for choosing our Houston TX air conditioning HVAC repair service."
- Location: only when the reviewer mentions it OR there's a natural opening. "Always great
  helping homeowners in Katy stay cool." Skip it if forced.
- Business name ("75 Degree AC"): use in 1 of every 4–5 replies, only when it flows. The
  name already appears next to every reply — no SEO lift from repeating it every time.
- **Test:** Remove the keyword/location and re-read. If the reply still makes sense, you've
  placed it naturally. If it only works because of the keyword, cut it.

---

## Staff Name Mentions

If a reviewer mentions a technician by name:
- Always call out that name in the reply — it's the strongest humanization signal available
- "We'll make sure [Name] sees this" or "[Name]'s going to love hearing that"
- Never fabricate a name if none was given

Known tech names from reviews: **Kenny** (mentioned multiple times)

---

## Sign-Off Rotation

Vary across these — don't use the same one twice in a week:

- `— The 75 Degree AC Team`
- `Thanks again, [reviewer name]!`
- (no sign-off — just end with a warm sentence)
- `Appreciate you, [name].`
- `See you next time — hopefully not in a crisis! 😄` (only when casual fits)

---

## Emoji Policy

- One emoji per reply max, only when it fits the tone naturally
- Never use: 🙏 (overused AI tell), ⭐, ❤️
- Acceptable: 😄 (humor/warmth), 😊 (genuine warmth), 🔧 (tech context, sparingly)
- If unsure, skip the emoji — absence is better than forced

---

## Positive Review Reply Checklist

Before publishing any reply, verify:

- [ ] Opens with reviewer's first name (not "Thank you for…")
- [ ] References at least one specific detail from their review
- [ ] No banned phrases present
- [ ] 40–100 words
- [ ] No phone number, URL, or promotional CTA
- [ ] Contractions used (we're, it's, glad, etc.)
- [ ] Sign-off varies from the last reply
- [ ] Opener word doesn't repeat from last 3 replies
- [ ] Reads naturally when read aloud — if it sounds like an ad, rewrite it

## Full Worked Examples — Good vs. Bad

### ❌ Bad (AI-pattern)
> "Thank you for your valuable feedback, Natalie! We're so glad you had a great experience
> with our team. We strive to provide exceptional service to all our valued customers in Katy
> and surrounding areas. We look forward to serving you again in the future!"

**Problems:** Opens with banned phrase, uses "great experience", "strive to provide",
"valued customers", "look forward to serving you again." Generic, robotic, zero specificity.

### ✓ Good
> "Natalie, so glad we could get to you fast — Houston heat waits for no one! The team will
> love hearing they made an impression. Better-than-ever cooling is exactly the goal. Come
> back anytime."

**Why it works:** Name first, mirrors her specific mention (quick response), one natural
Houston reference, warm close, no banned phrases, conversational.

---

### ❌ Bad
> "Bao, thank you for your amazing review! We are incredibly grateful for your kind words
> about Kenny. Our team of experts is dedicated to providing outstanding HVAC services in
> Houston TX. We appreciate your business and hope to serve you again!"

**Problems:** "incredibly grateful", "team of experts", "outstanding", keyword stuff, robotic.

### ✓ Good
> "Bao, Kenny's going to love seeing his name come up again! 😄 Quick and precise is exactly
> how he operates — really glad the job went smoothly. Thank you for the 5 stars."

**Why it works:** Specific name callout, mirrors his exact words ("quick and precise"),
light emoji that fits naturally, clean short close.

---

## Workflow When Asked to Write Review Replies

1. Read every review fully before writing any reply
2. Note: reviewer first name, specific detail(s) they mentioned, any staff names, location
   references, service type
3. Draft reply following the structure and rules above
4. Run the checklist before outputting
5. Vary openers and sign-offs across all replies in the same batch
6. Output replies clearly labeled by reviewer name
